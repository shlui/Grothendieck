## 1. Gaussian and tensor algebra

For a symmetric cubic tensor define the flattening
\(F_{ab,c}=f_{abc}\), and set

\[
A=FF^T,\quad G=F^TF,\quad S=\operatorname{Sym}_4(A),\quad
s=\|A\|_F^2=\|G\|_F^2,\quad h=\|S\|_F^2,
\quad V=\operatorname{tr}A^3,\quad V_4=\operatorname{tr}A^4.
\]

Here \(6\|f\|^2=1\). Full symmetrization is an orthogonal
projection, so \(\langle A,S\rangle=h\), \(0\le h\le s\), and

\[
\|S+tA\|_F^2=t^2s+(1+2t)h.
\tag{2}
\]

The Gaussian product formula yields

\[
U=3I_4(S)+12I_2(G),\quad
\mathbb E U^2=216h+288s,\quad
\kappa=1296s+1944h.
\tag{3}
\]

The derivative formula \(D_iI_3(f)=3I_2(f_i)\), followed by
\(I_2(g)^2=I_4(\operatorname{Sym}(g\otimes g))+
4I_2(g\otimes_1g)+2\|g\|^2\), proves the first identity.
Orthogonality of different Hermite degrees proves its variance.

The exact sixth-cumulant identity is

\[
\frac{\kappa_6}{46656}
=\frac{135}{2}\operatorname{tr}S^3
 +45\operatorname{tr}(AS^2)
 +\frac{165}{2}\operatorname{tr}(A^2S)
 -20\operatorname{tr}A^3
 +\frac{135}{2}\langle S,G\otimes G\rangle.
\tag{4}
\]

In addition to checking the retained formula, this work verified both
the fourth-cumulant formula in (3) and (4) by a dimension-free exact
connected-Wick-diagram enumeration. For \(r\) copies of a symmetric
cubic Wick tensor, a loopless multigraph \(m_{ij}\) with degree three
at each vertex has coefficient
\((3!)^r/\prod_{i<j}m_{ij}!\). Its vertices carry copies of \(f\),
and its edges contract their tensor indices. Cumulants retain precisely
the connected graphs. This follows directly by differentiating the
logarithm of the Wick exponential generating function: the exponential
formula removes disconnected diagrams.

`certify_wick_identity.py` enumerates these graphs and independently
expands each term on the right side of (3) and (4), using the three
pairings in the symmetrization of \(A\). It compares the rational
coefficient of every graph isomorphism class. The results are:

|---|---:|---:|---:|

Both comparisons pass exactly. The graph enumeration is independent

## 2. Explicit scalar upper bounds

Since \(s>0\), set
\[
y=\sqrt{h/s},\qquad a=(V/s^{3/2})^{1/3},\qquad
d=1+\frac32y^2.
\]
Then \(0\le y,a\le1\), \(\kappa=1296sd\), and
\[
\frac{\mathbb E U^2}{\kappa}=\frac19\left(1+\frac1d\right).
\tag{5}
\]
The range \(1\le d\le5/2\) proves the variance range above.
Since \(s\le(\operatorname{tr}A)^2=1/36\), (3) also gives
\(\kappa\le90\).

For one of the four pairs
\[
(p,q)=(17/33,-1),\ (62/105,-3/4),\ (28/39,-1/2),\ (3/4,-1),
\]
put
\[
c_p=\frac{55/2-15q}{p(p-q)},\quad
c_q=\frac{55/2-15p}{q(q-p)},\quad
\zeta=135/2-c_p-c_q,\quad
\beta=20+c_pp^3+c_qq^3.
\]
These selected pairs have \(c_p,c_q>0\), \(\zeta\ge0\).
Expansion gives the exact completion
\[
\frac{135}{2}\operatorname{tr}S^3+45\operatorname{tr}(AS^2)
+\frac{165}{2}\operatorname{tr}(A^2S)-20V
=c_p\operatorname{tr}(S+pA)^3+c_q\operatorname{tr}(S+qA)^3
 +\zeta\operatorname{tr}S^3-\beta V.
\]
For every real symmetric matrix, \(\operatorname{tr}B^3\le\|B\|_F^3\).
Furthermore
\[
\|\operatorname{Sym}_4(G\otimes G)\|_F^2=(s^2+2V_4)/3,
\quad V_4\le V^{4/3}.
\]
Applying these facts to (2) and (4) proves
\(\kappa_6/\kappa^{3/2}\le\mathcal J_{p,q}(y,a)/d^{3/2}\), where
\[
\mathcal J_{p,q}
=c_p[p^2+(1+2p)y^2]^{3/2}
+c_q[q^2+(1+2q)y^2]^{3/2}
+\zeta y^3-\beta a^3
+\frac{135}{2}y\sqrt{(1+2a^4)/3}.
\tag{6}
\]

Another valid bound is \(\mathcal M/d^{3/2}\), where
\[
\begin{split}
\mathcal M={}&\frac{135}{2}y^3+
\frac{135}{2}y\sqrt{(1+2a^4)/3}+45ay^2-20a^3\\
&+\frac{165}{2}\min\{a^{3/2}y,
y^2a^3+ya^2\sqrt{(1-y^2)(1-a^2)}\}.
\end{split}
\tag{7}
\]
The first mixed bound uses
\(\operatorname{tr}(A^2S)\le\sqrt{hV_4}\),
\(V_4\le\sqrt{s}V\). The second writes
\(R=S-(h/s)A\), so \(\langle A,R\rangle=0\), and applies
Cauchy--Schwarz to
\(\langle A^2-(V/s)A,R\rangle\), followed by \(V_4\le V^{4/3}\).
Thus neither (6) nor (7) assumes positivity of \(S\).

## 3. The exact finite scalar certificate

The fresh certificate proves throughout the closed unit square
\[
\frac{\min\{\mathcal M,\mathcal J_{17/33,-1},
\mathcal J_{62/105,-3/4},\mathcal J_{28/39,-1/2},
\mathcal J_{3/4,-1}\}}{d^{3/2}}
+\frac{211}{3d}\le\frac{194}{\sqrt{10}}+\frac{422}{15}.
\tag{8}
\]

Write \(x=1-y\), \(z=1-a\). Each leaf records a closed rational
rectangle in \((x,z)\), a selected bound, and rational tangent
parameters. Square roots are majorized by
\[
\sqrt X\le\frac{X+t^2}{2t},\qquad
\sqrt{XY}\le\frac{tX+Y/t}{2},\quad t>0.
\]
For the terms \(X^{3/2}\), multiply the first inequality by
\(X\ge0\). For rational \(y_0\in[0,1]\) and
\(l_0^2\ge1+3y_0^2/2\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives the lower bound
\[
\sqrt d\ge L(y)=\frac{1+3y_0y/2}{l_0}.
\]
The bracket \([(194/\sqrt{10}+422/15)d-211/3]\) is positive:
its minimum is \(194/\sqrt{10}-211/5>0\), as can be verified by
squaring the two positive sides. It therefore suffices to prove that
the selected polynomial majorant is at most this bracket times
\(L(y)\).

The single corner rectangle has \(x,z\in[0,2^{-19}]\), uses
\((p,q)=(3/4,-1)\), and uses
\(L(y)=(2+3y)/\sqrt{10}\). Here
\((1-y^2)^{3/2}\le(1-y^2)/512\); the other tangent parameters
are \(7/4\) and \(1\). These choices preserve equality at the
corner rather than losing it to rational approximation.

Every difference polynomial has degree at most \((4,4)\), with
coefficients in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{10})\). Substituting the affine
rectangle coordinates gives power coefficients \(c_{kl}\). The
Bernstein coefficients are
\[
b_{ij}=\sum_{k\le i,l\le j}c_{kl}
\frac{\binom{i}{k}}{\binom4k}
\frac{\binom{j}{l}}{\binom4l},\qquad0\le i,j\le4.
\]
All 25 coefficients are nonnegative on every leaf. The exact sign of
\(r+s\sqrt{10}\) is checked by rational sign cases and comparisons
of \(r^2\) with \(10s^2\). Nonnegativity of the Bernstein basis
then proves each leaf's comparison.

## 4. Obtaining the coupled inequality

Combine (5)--(8) and use
\[
633\left(\frac19\left(1+\frac1d\right)-\frac7{45}\right)
=\frac{211}{3d}-\frac{422}{15}.
\]
This gives (1). At the single Hermite polynomial, \(y=a=1\),
\(\kappa=90\), \(\kappa_6=52380\), and the deficit vanishes;
\((194/\sqrt{10})90^{3/2}=52380\).
