# Independent review of the universal reduced sequence

The proposition in `tonight/paper_universal_sequence.tex` passes conceptual audit. For each n the set of nonzero rectangular matrices with both dimensions at most n and entries in the stated dyadic grid is finite. The grids and dimension bounds are nested, so maximizing the ratio exactly gives a monotone sequence of ratios. Every nonzero matrix has positive scalar norm: for example sign orthogonality gives C(A)^2 at least the sum of squared entries. Both scalar and vector norms are Lipschitz with constant one for total entry error, so the ratio is continuous at any fixed nonzero matrix. Homogeneity and dyadic density therefore imply convergence to the full real Grothendieck supremum.

The vector norm is exactly the maximum of a rational linear objective over the compact real Gram spectrahedron with diagonal one. Positive semidefiniteness admits a finite polynomial description using principal minors. Real quantifier elimination therefore makes exact algebraic optimization and comparisons possible, including ties. This is a finite mathematical specification, not a claim of practical computational complexity or an effective convergence rate.

Clearing dyadic denominators and applying the already proved exact integer cloning/balancing maps leaves the ratio unchanged, so the resulting simultaneous reduced matrices retain the same monotonicity and limit. Their dimensions need not stay below n after reduction; the proposition does not claim that. A notation change from U_n avoids collision with later operator/Stein variables if desired.

No actual candidate enumeration or numerical lower-bound improvement is implied by this existence-and-computability theorem. The analytic cubic sequence remains a separately quantified subfamily.
