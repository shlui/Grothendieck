# What the recovered cubic cap can and cannot imply by itself

The accepted premise for this note is the dimension-uniform bound

\[
T_0=P_1-\frac{699}{1000}P_3,
\qquad N(T_0)\le M:=\frac{1000}{1773}.
\]

It is **not** a bound for the exploratory operator
\(1.008P_1-.699P_3-.008P_{\rm odd}\). The source correction is essential.
No new lower bound is asserted here.

## A feasible point that blocks a black-box improvement

For bounded odd real functions \(f,g\), introduce their individual Hermite
energies \(e_j^f,e_j^g\) and signed cross-correlations
\(c_j=\langle P_jf,P_jg\rangle\). A common relaxation retains:

* \(\sum_j e_j^f=\sum_j e_j^g=1\), nonnegative energies, and
  \(|c_j|\le\sqrt{e_j^f e_j^g}\);
* the exact first-level bound \(e_1^f,e_1^g\le2/\pi\);
* the baseline inequality \(|c_1-.699c_3|\le M\);
* the seven-copy phase inequalities for the two energy sequences;
* any finite list of positive Gaussian noise correlations and their
  Borell upper bounds.

This relaxation has the following feasible point. Choose an odd degree
\(L\ge9\), beyond every degree in a proposed finite head, and set

\[
e_1^f=e_1^g=c_1=M,\quad
e_L^f=e_L^g=1-M,\quad c_L=0,
\]

with all remaining energies and correlations zero. This is a genuine
positive semidefinite **formal Hilbert-space Gram configuration**: the
first-level vectors agree, and the two remaining vectors are orthogonal.
It is not claimed to come from a pair of Gaussian signs.

The odd-identity correlation is the sum of the correlations, hence
\(\langle f,g\rangle=M\). For an arbitrary finite-head operator plus a
constant odd tail,

\[
S=\sum_{j\in J}a_jP_j+\delta P_{\rm odd},\qquad 1\in J,\ L>\max J,
\]

the relaxed pairing is exactly

\[
\langle f,Sg\rangle=(a_1+\delta)M.
\]

Thus this relaxation cannot certify a scalar cap strictly below
\((a_1+\delta)M\). A radial vector sequence whose limiting numerator is
\(a_1+\delta>0\) cannot be certified beyond **1.773** using these
constraints alone. This covers both positive and negative odd-identity
corrections, arbitrary finite higher-chaos corrections, and linear dual
combinations of the listed inequalities.

## Why the point passes the additional checks

Write \(u=\cos(\pi/7)\), \(v=\sin(\pi/14)\). The full seven-copy bound is

\[
7\sum_{j\text{ odd}}(u^j-v^j)e_j\le4.
\]

Exact trigonometric enclosures give \(u<901/1000\) and
\(v>222/1000\). For every \(L\ge9\), its left side at the formal point is
at most

\[
7M\frac{679}{1000}+7(1-M)\left(\frac{901}{1000}\right)^9
<3.875008<4.
\]

For a fixed noise parameter \(0<r<1\), the individual formal noise
correlation is \(Mr+(1-M)r^L\). Borell's odd-sign cap is
\((2/\pi)\arcsin r\), which is at least \((2/\pi)r\). Because
\(M<2/\pi\), the formal point passes this bound whenever

\[
(1-M)r^{L-1}\le 2/\pi-M.
\]

For **any finite list** of parameters below one, a sufficiently large odd
\(L\) satisfies all these conditions. The replay checks a specific list
through \(r=9999/10000\), using \(L=20001\). The claim here is about
finite lists; it does not claim that a single finite-degree point passes
the entire continuum up to \(r=1\).

The familiar support decomposition also fails to exclude the point. For
\(h=(f+g)/2\), \(k=(f-g)/2\), its formal squared norms are
\(m_h=(1+M)/2\) and \(m_k=(1-M)/2\), and its first-level energies are
\(M\) and zero. The sharp fixed-mass first-moment cap for \(h\) is above
\(M\): its tail threshold is below \(3/10\), so the cap is at least
\((2/\pi)e^{-9/100}>(2/\pi)(91/100)>M\). The replay verifies this
without numerical Gaussian quadrature. The zero first-level energy of
\(k\) trivially passes its fixed-mass cap.

The previously proved cubic cap at coefficient \(.697\), the refined
globally bounded cubic at coefficient \(.50723\), and the published
Song–Yue positive-identity cubic cap also permit the point. For the last
operator its formal pairing is approximately \(.812356\), below the
published cap approximately \(.813142\).

## Consequence for the next proof

This is a limitation of the stated relaxation, **not** a family ceiling
for actual matrices and **not** an upper bound on \(G_{\mathbb R}\).
To improve the recovered endpoint, a proof has to use an additional
cross-level condition satisfied by actual bounded Gaussian functions,
such as the recovered conditional-law/amplitude cover. Repeating the
same black-box operator caps with phase, energy, or finitely many noise
inequalities cannot provide the missing strict improvement.

`certify_joint_cap_limitation.py` is an independent, standard-library,
exact-arithmetic replay. Its JSON record is a certificate of the
relaxation limitation only.
