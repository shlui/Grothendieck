# Exact physical source polygon audit

The helper `tonight/theory/source_polygon_prices.py` is mathematically valid for the rational polygons actually generated by the envelope. Every source plane must first have a uniform proved cap on the whole cumulant and shape band. The independently checked contact rows have positive sextic coefficients; their moment prices are convex in the cumulant and affine in shape, so four directed endpoint prices suffice for each fixed row. It is valid to intersect several such planes, even when different rows produce the different planes.

`clip_source_polygon` starts from the closed cell rectangle and applies exact rational half-plane intersections. The sign convention retains the plane boundary. The transition formula `fp/(fp-fc)` is the exact segment intersection. Consecutive duplicate elimination preserves point and segment intersections; only a truly empty intersection can be discarded.

For the price

\[
f(u,r)=a u^2+b u+c r^2+d r+e,\qquad a>0,
\]

a two-dimensional interior point cannot maximize on a bounded polygon: on a horizontal line through that point, the strictly convex quadratic in `u` has a larger value at one of the boundary endpoints. Degenerate polygons are already their own boundary. Along each edge the price is a univariate quadratic. A convex or affine edge price is bounded by its endpoints; a concave edge price can have an interior maximum, which the helper explicitly computes and checks. Thus the rational maximum returned by `max_source_quadratic` is exact. Checking only polygon vertices would be incorrect; the included example has a maximum `5/4` at `(1/2,1/2)`, larger than its vertex prices.

The native source target expectation is convex in `r` because the frozen nodal target is convex and nondecreasing. Upper bounds at radial grid points therefore yield source chords. Different globally valid scalar rows may be used at different radial endpoints. At each endpoint, however, the selected scalar/L1 row is fixed across its four cumulant/shape corners; the independent native replay verifies this. The final native price is the source chord plus `g*r^2+u^2-2*ell*u`. It must be split at **every radial knot** before polygon maximization. The production wrapper `polygon_prices.py` performs that split and rebuilds the clipped polygon in each subinterval.

Fixed nonnegative contact multipliers add only linear terms in `u,r` and a constant, so the same maximum theorem applies. Optimizing a multiplier separately at sampled points does not replace a uniform proof; the saved envelope uses one exact fixed multiplier per selected whole-cell option. A floating nomination can choose any valid option, since the complete proof needs an upper bound rather than proof that the chosen option is the optimal minimum. Every actual nominated option must nevertheless be independently repriced, and every original source cell must be retained or rigorously excluded.

For the coarse eleven-profile polygon envelope with SHA256 `4fffacd3a4bc1e47ef719e13d4d0c6cc24e1ecead47603ec19001e4206543c7e`, the numeric premises freshly pass: 616 R3 corner prices, 560 uniform source-plane corner prices, and 250 forward-certified physical inverse caps. The exact exhaustive cell partition, selected option reconstruction, and final bound are separately audited by the theory agent. This note does not itself promote that envelope to a global bound.
