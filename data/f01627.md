# A rigorous barrier for the scalar degree-six moment relaxation

The second, fourth and sixth moments, even with the full stated `633` deficit coupling and the complete allowed Stein-variance range, do **not** imply the desired uniform first-absolute-moment bound. The obstruction is an exact discrete symmetric law. It is a counterexample to the relaxation, not a counterexample in third Gaussian chaos.

The target is \(\mathbb E|Q|\le0.8845\), or more precisely \((\mathbb E|Q|)^2\le500/639\), whose square-root is about \(0.884574820724\).

## Exact rational obstruction, valid across the entire variance range

Let \(X\) be symmetric, with

\[
\Pr\{|X|=9/10\}=\frac{2400}{2419},\qquad
\Pr\{|X|=5\}=\frac{19}{2419}.
\]

Each sign has half the displayed probability. Direct rational arithmetic gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EX^2&=1,\\
\mathbb EX^4&=139/25=3+\kappa,\quad \kappa=64/25,\\
\mathbb EX^6&=154067/1250,\\
\kappa_6(X)&=\mathbb EX^6-15\mathbb EX^4+30=87317/1250,\\
\mathbb E|X|&=55/59=0.9322033898305\ldots.
\end{aligned}
\]

For every numerical variance parameter \(V\in[7\kappa/45,2\kappa/9]\),

\[
\frac{194}{\sqrt{10}}\kappa^{3/2}
-633\sqrt\kappa\left(V-\frac{7\kappa}{45}\right)
\ge\left(\frac{194}{\sqrt{10}}-\frac{211}{5}\right)\kappa^{3/2}.
\]

Here \(\sqrt\kappa=8/5\). The elementary inequality \(\sqrt{10}<16/5\) implies

\[
\left(\frac{194}{\sqrt{10}}-\frac{211}{5}\right)\kappa^{3/2}
>\frac{737}{40}\frac{512}{125}
=\frac{47168}{625}
=\frac{87317}{1250}+\frac{7019}{1250}.
\]

Thus the law satisfies even the most restrictive endpoint of the entire given deficit family, with exact rational slack at least \(7019/1250\). Nevertheless

\[
\mathbb E|X|=55/59>0.8845,\qquad
(\mathbb E|X|)^2=3025/3481>500/639.
\]

No conclusion about an actual third-chaos Stein variable is made: the example only establishes that the listed numerical moment inequalities, including their variance parameter, are insufficient by themselves. Additional identities tying the full law to its Stein kernel may exclude it.

## Exact optimum of the three-moment relaxation

For a fixed fourth moment \(s>1\) and sixth-moment upper bound \(\tau>s^2\), put

\[
S=\frac{\tau-s}{s-1},\qquad P=S-s,
\quad a,b=\frac{S\mp\sqrt{S^2-4P}}2.
\]

Then \(0<a<1<b\). The two-point law for \(T=X^2\), with probabilities

\[
\Pr\{T=a\}=\frac{b-1}{b-a},\qquad
\Pr\{T=b\}=\frac{1-a}{b-a},
\]

satisfies \(\mathbb ET=1\), \(\mathbb ET^2=s\), and \(\mathbb ET^3=\tau\). Its first absolute moment is

\[
\boxed{\quad M(s,\tau)=\frac{1+\sqrt P}{\sqrt{S+2\sqrt P}}.\quad}
\]

This is the **exact maximum** over all real laws with those scalar moment constraints, not merely a sampled lower bound. To prove the upper bound, write \(u=\sqrt a\), \(v=\sqrt b\), \(D=2uv(u+v)^3\), and form the cubic polynomial in \(t\)

\[
\begin{aligned}
D p(t)={}&t^3-(2u^2+uv+2v^2)t^2\\
&+(u^4+3u^3v+7u^2v^2+3uv^3+v^4)t\\
&+u^2v^2(u^2+3uv+v^2).
\end{aligned}
\]

For every \(x\ge0\), the exact factorization

\[
D[p(x^2)-x]
=(x-u)^2(x-v)^2
\big[x^2+2(u+v)x+(u+v)^2+uv\big]\ge0
\]

proves a whole-line degree-six majorant of \(|X|\). The coefficient of \(t^3\) is positive, so substituting \(\mathbb EX^6\le\tau\) gives the claimed maximum. Equality holds for the two-node law. Symmetrizing it provides zero odd moments without changing the objective.

For the weaker sharp cumulant cap alone, take

\[
s=3+\kappa,\qquad
\tau=15+15\kappa+\frac{194}{\sqrt{10}}\kappa^{3/2}.
\]

The condition \(\tau>s^2\) holds on \([0,90]\), since
\(\tau-s^2=6+9\kappa+\kappa^{3/2}(194/\sqrt{10}-\sqrt\kappa)>0\).
The entire moment relaxation therefore has the explicit optimum above. Numerical evaluation, for discovery only, gives a maximum around \(0.972791\), at \(\kappa\approx4.2763\). The exact counterexample already proves failure of the desired cap, so this numerical maximum is not needed by the argument.

## The Gaussian endpoint itself already exhibits loss

At \(\kappa=0\), take \(s=3\), \(\tau=15\). Then \(a,b=3\mp\sqrt6\), and

\[
M(3,15)^2=\frac{3+\sqrt3}{6},\qquad
M(3,15)=0.8880738339771\ldots.
\]

The elementary comparison \(\sqrt3>17/10\) yields \(M(3,15)^2>47/60>500/639\). In genuine normalized third chaos, \(\kappa=0\) imposes further Gaussian structure; the scalar degree-six relaxation does not retain that fact. One can avoid this endpoint issue entirely by using the positive-cumulant rational example above.
