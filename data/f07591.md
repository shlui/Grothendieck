# Gaussian-to-matrix transfer: independent audit and a sharper error bound

The cubic radial projection formulas and the product-cell transfer are
correct. A new elementary error estimate halves the leading midpoint
error constant and avoids the separate small-radius probability bound.
This is a numerator/approximation improvement; it supplies no missing
global scalar norm certificate.

## 1. Exact radial vector energies

Let \(X\sim\gamma_d\), \(R=\|X\|\), and
\(V_i(X)=X_i/R\) off the null origin. Put

\[
a_d=\frac{(\mathbb ER)^2}{d}
=\frac2d\left[\frac{\Gamma((d+1)/2)}{\Gamma(d/2)}\right]^2.
\]

Rotational symmetry gives
\(P_1V_i=(\mathbb ER/d)X_i\), hence
\(\sum_i\|P_1V_i\|_2^2=a_d\).
The unique rotationally equivariant pure degree-three vector polynomial
is \(W_i=X_i(R^2-d-2)\). It obeys

\[
\|W_i\|_2^2=2(d+2),\qquad
\langle V_i,W_i\rangle=-\frac{\mathbb ER}{d}.
\]

For the inner product, use isotropy and
\(\mathbb ER^3=(d+1)\mathbb ER\):
\(\langle V_i,W_i\rangle=
[\mathbb ER^3-(d+2)\mathbb ER]/d\).
Thus

\[
P_3V_i=-\frac{\mathbb ER}{2d(d+2)}W_i,
\qquad
\boxed{\sum_i\|P_3V_i\|_2^2=\frac{a_d}{2(d+2)}.}
\tag{1}
\]

The gamma recurrence gives
\(a_{d+2}/a_d=(d+1)^2/[d(d+2)]\).
Together with \(a_d\to1\), it implies
\(a_d>1-1/(2d)\): along each parity,
the two-step ratio of \(a_d/(1-1/(2d))\) equals
\(1-1/[d^2(2d+3)]<1\) and tends to one.
Convergence follows directly from concentration of \(R^2/d\),
uniform integrability of \(R/\sqrt d\), and \(\mathbb ER^2/d=1\).

For \(T=\alpha P_1-\epsilon P_3+\tau P_{\rm odd}\), the radial
vector objective is exactly

\[
q_d=a_d\left(\alpha-\frac{\epsilon}{2(d+2)}\right)+\tau.
\tag{2}
\]

The last term is exactly \(\tau\), because every coordinate of
\(V\) is odd and \(\sum_iV_i^2=1\). For the pure candidate
\(P_1-(18/25)P_3\), (2) tends to one. For the summed-tail
candidate \((126/125)P_1-(699/1000)P_3-(1/125)P_{\rm odd}\),
it also tends to one.

## 2. A sharper normalized midpoint bound

Take \(d=n^2>2\), product mesh \(h=1/n^2\) inside \([-n,n]\),
and two tails per coordinate. Assign each interior interval its
midpoint and each tail its clipped endpoint \(\pm n\). Let
\(r(X)\) be that product representative and
\(v(X)=r(X)/\|r(X)\|\). With a partition at integer multiples of
the mesh, each midpoint has nonzero coordinates and \(r(X)\ne0\).
The assignment extends oddly across antipodal cells.

Let \(E=\{\max_i|X_i|>n\}\). Off \(E\),
\(\|r-X\|^2\le dh^2/4\), and the elementary normalization
inequality gives
\[
\left\|\frac r{\|r\|}-\frac X{\|X\|}\right\|
\le\frac{2\|r-X\|}{\|X\|}.
\]
On \(E\), the difference is at most two. Consequently
\[
\mathbb E\|v-V\|^2
\le dh^2\mathbb ER^{-2}+4\Pr(E).
\]
The chi-square density gives \(\mathbb ER^{-2}=1/(d-2)\).
The union bound and Gaussian Mills inequality give
\(\Pr(E)\le2d\overline\Phi(n)\le2d\phi(n)/n\).
Therefore

\[
\boxed{\quad
\|v-V\|_{L^2(\gamma_d;\mathbb R^d)}\le
\varepsilon_n:=
\sqrt{\frac1{n^2(n^2-2)}+8n\phi(n)}.
\quad}
\tag{3}
\]

This is valid for every integer \(n\ge2\) with the stated partition.
Its leading order is
\(\varepsilon_n=n^{-2}+O(n^{-4})\). The earlier radius-splitting
bound had leading term \(2n^{-2}\). There is no independence
assumption between the midpoint error and the radius: the deterministic
error bound off \(E\) is integrated against \(R^{-2}\).

## 3. Finite matrices and the objective error

For folded antipodal cells \(U\), put
\(p_U=2\gamma_d(U)\),
\(F_j[U,\xi]=2\int_U H_\xi\,d\gamma_d\) for \(|\xi|=j\).
The exact finite matrix is
\[
A_n=\alpha F_1F_1^T-\epsilon F_3F_3^T
+\tau\operatorname{diag}(p).
\tag{4}
\]
Its midpoint-vector objective equals the continuum objective of the
odd cell-constant field \(v\). The diagonal term is essential when
\(\tau\ne0\): its contribution is exactly \(\tau\), rather
than an ordinary averaged two-variable kernel.

For a bounded selfadjoint operator \(S\), unit \(L^2\) vector
fields satisfy
\[
|\langle v,Sv\rangle-\langle V,SV\rangle|
\le2\|S\|_{2\to2}\|v-V\|_2.
\]
Both \(v\) and \(V\) are odd unit fields. Consequently subtracting
**any** multiple \(\lambda P_{\rm odd}\) leaves the difference
of their two objectives unchanged. Choose \(\lambda\) to be the
midpoint of the odd spectral range. Its shifted operator norm is half
the spectral diameter. Thus the stronger conclusion is
\[
\boxed{\quad Q(A_n)\ge q_{n^2}-\Delta\varepsilon_n,\qquad
\Delta=\max\{\alpha,-\epsilon,0\}
-\min\{\alpha,-\epsilon,0\}.\quad}
\tag{5}
\]
The odd correction \(\tau\) cancels from the diameter. For positive
\(\alpha,\epsilon\), \(\Delta=\alpha+\epsilon\).
For \(P_1-.72P_3\), the error multiplier is \(1.72\).
For the specified summed-tail candidate it is \(1.707\).
For the Song--Yue positive odd correction it is \(\alpha+1\).
This shift was identified in the independent transfer audit by the
`finite_search` agent; it strengthens the unshifted bound without any
new scalar-norm assumption.

In particular, for \(b=18/25\),
\[
Q(A_n)\ge
\left(1-\frac1{2n^2}\right)
\left(1-\frac{b}{2(n^2+2)}\right)-(1+b)\varepsilon_n.
\tag{6}
\]
The compression is a contraction on \(L^2\), so its vector objective
is also at most the full operator norm one. Thus \(Q(A_n)\to1\).

The scalar objective is exactly the original operator pairing of two
odd cell-constant signs. Therefore any genuinely dimension-uniform
all-sign bound \(C(T)\le B\) implies \(C(A_n)\le B\).
A regional amplitude bound cannot be used as \(B\) in this step.
Rational entry approximation adds precisely its total entrywise
\(\ell^1\) error to each norm bound; exact cloning, balancing and
reflection operations retain the resulting ratio as proved earlier.

## 4. Varying dimension and nonnested cells

Let \(N_*\) be the supremum of the operator's scalar sign norm over
finite Gaussian dimensions. The product partitions need not be nested.
For any fixed-dimensional sign pair, embed it by ignoring the remaining
coordinates and approximate it by bounded functions on the first few
coordinates of the grid. Such cell approximations converge in \(L^2\)
as mesh tends to zero and cutoff tends to infinity. Odd projection and
convex averaging into odd signs preserve the norm supremum. Boundedness
of the operator makes the pairings converge. Since the available
dimension eventually exceeds that fixed dimension, this proves
\(\liminf C(A_n)\ge N_*\), while the opposite inequality holds for
every \(n\). Hence
\(C(A_n)\to N_*\) and the actual matrix ratios tend to \(1/N_*\)
for the two unit-top-eigenvalue candidates above. This identifies the
sequence's limit without pretending that the missing global norm has
been evaluated.

## 5. A stronger quadratic objective-error bound

The unit-vector field gives a further improvement beyond (5).
Assume \(\epsilon\ge0\), \(\alpha\ge\epsilon/2\), as for all
current cubic candidates. Put \(\eta=v-V\). Since both fields have
unit length pointwise,
\(2\langle\eta,V\rangle=-\|\eta\|^2\).
The exact radial projections above give
\[
T V=t(R)V,\qquad
t(R)=\frac{\mathbb ER}{d}R
\left(\alpha-\frac\epsilon2+
\frac{\epsilon R^2}{2(d+2)}\right)+\tau.
\]
On the odd subspace the smallest eigenvalue of \(T\) is
\(\tau-\epsilon\). Expanding the quadratic objective therefore
gives
\[
\begin{split}
\langle v,Tv\rangle-q_d
&=2\langle\eta,TV\rangle+\langle\eta,T\eta\rangle\\
&\ge-\mathbb E[W(R)\|\eta\|^2],\\
W(R)&=\frac{\mathbb ER}{d}R
\left(\alpha-\frac\epsilon2+
\frac{\epsilon R^2}{2(d+2)}\right)+\epsilon.
\end{split}
\tag{7}
\]
The weight is nonnegative. Off the tail event \(E\),
\(\|\eta\|^2\le dh^2/R^2\). Moreover
\(\mathbb ER\mathbb ER^{-1}=da_d/(d-1)\), by the gamma
recurrence, and \(\mathbb ER^{-2}=1/(d-2)\). Hence the off-tail
contribution is at most
\[
dh^2\left[
\frac{a_d(\alpha-\epsilon/2)}{d-1}
+\frac{\epsilon a_d}{2(d+2)}
+\frac\epsilon{d-2}\right].
\]
Replace \(a_d\le1\) to obtain a completely elementary upper bound.

Let \(p_n=\min\{1,2n\phi(n)\}\). On \(E\), the direction
difference is at most two. Cauchy--Schwarz and the radial moments
\(\mathbb ER^2=d\), \(\mathbb ER^6=d(d+2)(d+4)\), together
with \(\mathbb ER\le\sqrt d\), give
\[
4\mathbb E[W(R)\mathbf1_E]\le
4\left[\alpha-\frac\epsilon2+
\frac\epsilon2\sqrt{\frac{d+4}{d+2}}\right]\sqrt{p_n}
+4\epsilon p_n.
\]
Consequently, with \(d=n^2\),
\[
\boxed{\begin{split}
Q(A_n)&\ge q_{n^2}-\mathcal E_n,\\
\mathcal E_n&=
\frac1{n^2}\left[
\frac{\alpha-\epsilon/2}{n^2-1}
+\frac\epsilon{2(n^2+2)}
+\frac\epsilon{n^2-2}\right]\\
&\quad+4\left[\alpha-\frac\epsilon2+
\frac\epsilon2\sqrt{\frac{n^2+4}{n^2+2}}\right]\sqrt{p_n}
+4\epsilon p_n.
\end{split}}
\tag{8}
\]
This bound is uniform over the operator's odd correction \(\tau\),
which cancels. Its mesh contribution has leading order
\((\alpha+\epsilon)n^{-4}\), rather than the linear direction-error
order \(n^{-2}\). The continuum radial loss in (2) still has order
\(n^{-2}\). Thus the total numerator error now has that sharp radial
order with a smaller leading constant, while the discretization error
is two powers smaller.

The argument uses the exact pointwise alignment of \(TV\) with
\(V\), rather than assuming that \(V\) is a finite-dimensional SDP
optimizer. It makes no claim about the unknown scalar norm.
