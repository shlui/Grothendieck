The manuscript consistently distinguishes its fresh theorems, its inherited global denominator premise, and the still unresolved target. In particular, it does not claim a fresh lower endpoint of 1.775 or a three-significant-digit determination.

Two small scope/notation corrections are recommended before freezing the text:

1. **Computability of the coefficient (lines 155 and 172).** The construction starts with an arbitrary real `b in (0,1)` but calls its entries computable. An arbitrary real can be noncomputable. Specify “computable `b in (0,1)`,” or restrict the explicit construction to rational `b`. The identities and limiting variational statements hold for every real `b`; the effective matrix specification needs computable `b`. Both displayed candidates, `.699` and `.72`, are rational, so this does not change any claimed endpoint or candidate.
2. **Tensor symmetry wording (lines 346–348).** Replace “All tensors are real and symmetric” by “The kernel `f` is a real fully symmetric third-order tensor.” The flattened matrices `A=FF^T` and `G=F^TF` are symmetric matrices, but `A` is generally not fully symmetric as a four-index tensor; `S=Sym(A)` is its full symmetrization. Leaving the original sentence broad could suggest `h=s` identically and obscure the nontrivial tensor-shape parameter.

### PAQ, absolute stochastic cloning, and balanced lift

The disjoint-support aggregate argument gives the stated weights for both scalar boxes and vector balls. Each vertex or boundary direction is attained by aligned copies. Independent left/right orthogonal completion preserves all cross inner products when replacing vector balls by unit spheres.

For integer `Z`, the clone denominator `r_i c_j` makes the total row and column copying weights equal to one and makes every row and column of `|B|` sum to one. Thus `C(B)=C(Z)` and `V(B)=V(Z)` are correct; clearing a rational denominator preserves the ratio rather than the unscaled norms.

The symmetric off-diagonal lift `K(B)` has norm factor two, because its two variable-list pairs are independent. The signed-copy lift `J_0 tensor K(B)` has another factor two: the left and right copying absolute weights are each `sqrt(2)`. The factor four in both norms, zero diagonal, zero sums, and absolute doubly stochastic property are all consistent. The result remains bilinear; replacing it by a same-sign quadratic optimization would be a different problem, and the manuscript does not make that replacement.

### Radial field and quantitative finite transfer

The radial energies are `a_d` and `a_d/[2(d+2)]`. The radial operator image

`T_b w = (mu R/d) [1-b/2+b R^2/(2(d+2))] w`

has nonnegative multiplier for `0<b<1`. Consequently the unit-vector relation `e dot w=-|e|^2/2` cancels the first-order discretization error. Since the smallest spectral coefficient is `-b`, the loss is bounded by `E[(t(R)+b)|e|^2]`, with the sign shown in the manuscript.

The off-exterior midpoint bound, inverse-radius moment, radial sixth moment, and Gaussian union bound give the stated `L_n(b)` and its leading term `(1+b)n^-4`. The `d=n^2` grid is adequate for convergence in each fixed embedded dimension even though the partitions are not nested. Odd cell averaging and cube vertices imply `C(A_n)->N(b)`; vector norm one and the radial lower assignment imply `V(A_n)->1`.

The useful gamma lower bound can be made completely transparent with one extra algebraic line: if `b_d=1-1/(2d)`, then

`(a_(d+2)/b_(d+2))/(a_d/b_d) = 1 - 1/[d^2(2d+3)] < 1`.

Since both parity subsequences tend to one, `a_d>b_d`. The subsequent dyadic entry-error budgets and reciprocal ratio perturbation have the correct directions. Nonzero seeds follow from the cell-constant hemisphere sign and its value `(2/pi)(1-b/6)>1/4`.

### Diffuse cubic sign witness and original-degree accounting

The complete accounting in the witness README is sufficient. A bounded smooth sign approximation with sufficiently many bounded derivatives allows coordinatewise Taylor expansion. A transverse `H_1(X_i)` coefficient first appears at order `n^-3/2`; its first two candidate terms vanish by orthogonality and parity. A transverse `H_2(X_i)` coefficient is `O(n^-1)`. After summing squared coefficients, every original-degree-one/three basis type except pure `H_1(Z)`, pure `H_3(Z)`, and transverse `H_3(X_i)` disappears. The last type has coefficients `n^-1/2 m_Y+o(n^-1/2)` and retains total energy `m_Y^2`. Reflection changes its sign for `g`, but preserves the two pure-Z coefficients. This proves the pairings `m_1^2` and `m_3^2-m_Y^2`.

Removing the approximation is uniform in `n`: conditioning on the transverse sum and using the strictly increasing cubic threshold with derivative at least `511/200` bounds the sign-transition band by a constant times its width. Orthogonal projections are `L_2` contractions. Thus mere convergence in distribution is not being mistaken for convergence of original-chaos projections. The three displayed integrals follow from normal integration by parts for the continuous cubic threshold.

For a standalone paper, the sentence “full degree accounting is included in the source package” should name `reduced_search/operator/cubic720/README.md`, or the short nine-row accounting table could be moved into an appendix. This is a presentation recommendation rather than a missing mathematical argument in the package.
