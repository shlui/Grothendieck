# Joint Stein pricing and the quadratic matrix transfer

## 1. Actual native-profile convention and multiplier signs

The current `tonight/operator/native/native_stein_profile.py` uses a local
majorant `A(t)` that **excludes** the completion cost `β²/b`. Its profile
reduction is

\[
 F\le u^2-2\ell u+\mathbb E A(r|Q|)+G r^2-2\beta v-bv^2,
 \qquad v=\mathbb E[H_3(Z)h].
\]

The transverse source and support comparison give

\[
 au+dv+r\le\mathbb E J_{a,d}(Q),\qquad
 \mathbb E|Q|\le\rho(\kappa,V).
\]

For nonnegative multipliers `λ,μ`, both gaps can safely be added to an
upper bound. Consequently the common target is

\[
 H(x)=A(r|x|)+\lambda J_{a,d}(x)-\mu|x|,
\]

and the resulting outer expression has the constants

\[
 -\lambda(r+au)+\mu\rho(\kappa,V)+G r^2
 +\frac{(\beta+\lambda d/2)^2}{b}.
\]

The last sign follows by maximizing
`−b v²−(2β+λd)v` over the real value of `v`. It would be wrong to add a
second `β²/b` after this completion, or to use `β−λd/2`. A profile whose
local majorant already includes `β²/b` needs a different accounting.

The Gaussian reference mean of the common target is

\[
 m_H=\mathbb E A(r|N|)+\lambda m_J-\mu c,
 \qquad c=\sqrt{2/\pi},\quad m_J=\mathbb E J_{a,d}(N).
\]

Thus the code's term `lambda_L1*(rho−c)` and its positive
`lambda_Ja*source_mean` have the correct signs. For `d=0`,
`m_J=c sqrt(1+a²)`. The numerical cubic-head mean for `d>0` remains a
discovery value until separately enclosed with directed arithmetic.

## 2. Common Stein regression, including centered Young pricing

Let `f_H` be the odd Gaussian Stein solution

\[
 f_H'(x)-xf_H(x)=H(x)-m_H.
\]

For normalized pure third chaos `Q`, write

\[
 U=\|DQ\|^2/3-1,\qquad
 s(x)=x^2-1,\qquad
 \theta=\frac{\kappa}{3(\kappa+2)},\qquad U_c=U-\theta s(Q).
\]

The fixed-chaos Stein identity gives
`E[U s(Q)]=κ/3`, while `E s(Q)²=κ+2`. Hence

\[
 \mathbb E U_c=\mathbb E[U_c s(Q)]=0,\qquad
 V_c:=\mathbb E U_c^2=V-\frac{\kappa^2}{9(\kappa+2)},
 \qquad U_c\ge-L(Q),\quad L(x)=1+\theta s(x)>0.
\]

For an admissible odd regression `φ`, put `z=f_H'−φ'` and
`z_c=z−γ0−γ2 s`. The correct inequality is

\[
 \mathbb E H(Q)\le m_H+
 \mathbb E\!\left[\phi'(Q)-Q\phi(Q)-\theta s(Q)z(Q)
             +\Psi_{\eta,L(Q)}(z_c(Q))\right]
 +\frac\eta2 V_c,
\]

where

\[
 \Psi_{\eta,L}(t)=\sup_{w\ge-L}\{-wt-\eta w^2/2\}
 =\begin{cases}
 t^2/(2\eta),&t\le\eta L,\\
 Lt-\eta L^2/2,&t>\eta L.
 \end{cases}
\]

Only the Young residual may omit `γ0+γ2 s`; the explicit term
`−θ s z` must use the original residual. The present code follows that
convention. The SOC constraints implement the same clipped conjugate
via its nonnegative infimal-convolution slack.

If this whole-line integrand has a certified majorant
`q0+q2 x²+q4 x⁴+q6 x⁶`, with `q6≥0`, its moment price is

\[
 m_H+q_0+q_2+(3+\kappa)q_4
 +\left[15+15\kappa+
  \left(\frac{194}{\sqrt{10}}-\frac{633\zeta}{15}\right)
       \kappa^{3/2}\right]q_6
 +\frac\eta2\left[
   \kappa\left(\frac7{45}+\frac\zeta{15}\right)
   -\frac{\kappa^2}{9(\kappa+2)}\right],
 \quad 0\le\zeta\le1.
\]

Add the constants in Section 1 and `u²−2ℓu` to obtain the native outer
cap. The same `κ,V` must be used throughout.

For interval coverage in `κ`, use the actual rational function `θ(κ)`.
If instead a fitting-point value `θ0` is frozen, the claimed orthogonality
to `s(Q)` fails away from that point. One must then add the covariance
correction `−γ2[κ/3−θ0(κ+2)]` and use
`V−2θ0κ/3+θ0²(κ+2)` in the variance price. Pointwise fitted rows cannot
be copied unchanged over a cumulant interval.

## 3. Separate negative odd-moment target

For the distinct experiment

\[
 H(x)=-|x|^3-t|x|^5+\lambda J_a(x),\qquad t\ge0,
\]

the Gaussian mean and Stein derivative on `x≥0` are exactly

\[
 m_H=-c(2+8t)+\lambda c\sqrt{1+a^2},
\]
\[
 f_H'(x)=2x+t(4x^3+8x)+c(2+8t)G(x)+\lambda f_a'(x),
 \qquad G(x)=1-xR(x),
\]
\[
 f_a'(x)=-c\sqrt{1+a^2}\,G(x)
       +ca e^{-x^2/(2a^2)}G(\sqrt{1+a^2}\,x/a).
\]

This follows from the tail antiderivatives
`f_|x|³ = 2cR−(x²+2)` and
`f_|x|⁵ = 8cR−(x⁴+4x²+8)`. The positive polynomial terms in the
derivative are required; reusing the nonpositive `J_a` derivative bound
for this joint target is invalid.

Apply either ordinary Young or the centered formula above to this single
target. If it is used to upper-bound the native profile with a positive
source multiplier, the source subtraction constants from Section 1
still have to be included. A moment target by itself is not a native
operator objective.

## 4. Complete-halfline certification strategy

Freeze a rational target, regression, multipliers, `η`, and majorant before
acceptance. Reconstruct the Stein derivative from the full common target,
not a sum of separately optimized expectation prices. On the compact
half-line, directed Taylor or interval bounds can use `R'=xR−1` and the
explicit source derivative above. For the convex piecewise-linear native
majorant, split every compact cover at its rescaled hinges `t_i/r`;
the hinge Stein solution is only piecewise smooth to the high order needed
by Taylor tests. Its value and first derivative agree at each hinge.

Test the exact centered/clipped scalar expression. If its branch cannot
be selected on a box, use its globally larger quadratic Young value.
At each hinge and clipping boundary, retain closed interval coverage.
Evenness then covers the negative half-line.

For the unbounded tail, bound `0≤G(x)≤x^−2` and enclose the exponentially
small scaled source terms. Clear all positive rational denominators and
translate the resulting polynomial at a rational cutoff. Nonnegative
translated coefficients, or a finite exact Bernstein cover followed by
such a tail, prove the full unbounded domain. For the negative fifth-moment
target the residual has a cubic leading term; the tail check must retain
that term and cannot reuse a `J_a`-only tail certificate unchanged.

For `d>0`, directed enclosure of the cubic inverse, Gaussian source mean,
and Stein-tail integral is also necessary. Finite Laguerre/Hermite
quadrature convergence is a discovery diagnostic, not an acceptance rule.

After scalar acceptance, the operator still needs complete local-potential
coverage, parameter boxes in `r,κ,ζ,u`, exact SPD/PSD gates, and a valid
common-profile dispatch. Frozen rows at sampled parameters do not supply
those missing continuum conditions.

## 5. Quadratic radial transfer passes

The new Section 5 of `tonight/theory/gaussian_transfer_audit.md` and the
paper's `L_n(b)` pass independent derivation. The exact alignment is

\[
 TV=t(R)V,\quad
 t(R)=\frac{\mathbb ER}{d}R
 \left(\alpha-\epsilon/2+
       \frac{\epsilon R^2}{2(d+2)}\right)+\tau.
\]

For two pointwise unit fields, `2 e·V=−||e||²`. Combining this identity
with the lowest odd spectral value `τ−ε` gives the weighted quadratic
loss with weight `t−τ+ε`. It is nonnegative when `ε≥0` and
`α≥ε/2`, as for the current candidates. The odd correction `τ` cancels
exactly.

Off the coordinate-tail event, `||e||²≤d h²/R²`. The identities
`E R^−2=1/(d−2)` and `E R E R^−1=d a_d/(d−1)` yield the displayed
three mesh terms. On the tail, `||e||²≤4`, Cauchy–Schwarz with `E R²=d`
and `E R⁶=d(d+2)(d+4)` yields exactly the coefficient of `sqrt(p_n)`
and the additional `4εp_n`. No radius/error independence is assumed.

Thus the pure cubic paper's discretization loss is exactly its displayed
`L_n(b)`, and `L_n(b)=(1+b)n^−4+O(n^−6)`. The remaining continuum radial
loss is of order `n^−2`. Combining the rational entry budget `n^−4`
with any actually proved scalar cap `B` gives the stated finite ratio
and its leading loss `(1+b)/(2Bn²)`. The bound is valid for `n≥2` and
the current coefficients. It supplies no missing scalar norm cap.
