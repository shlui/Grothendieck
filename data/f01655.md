## Limiting sign construction

Let all Gaussian variables be independent, use normalized probabilists'
Hermite polynomials, with H3(x)=(x^3-3x)/sqrt(6), and set
\[
Y_n=n^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^n H_3(X_j),\quad
q(z)=\frac{511}{200}z+\frac{393}{1000}z^3,
\quad f_n=\operatorname{sgn}(Y_n+q(Z)),\quad
 g_n=\operatorname{sgn}(-Y_n+q(Z)).
\]
The central limit theorem gives \(Y_n\Rightarrow Y\sim N(0,1)\). The limiting
first-chaos pair is \(m_1^2\); the limiting third-chaos pair is
\(m_3^2-m_Y^2\), where
\[
 m_Y=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty e^{-(z^2+q(z)^2)/2}\,dz,
\quad m_1=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty q'(z)e^{-(z^2+q(z)^2)/2}\,dz,
\]
\[
 m_3=\frac2{\pi\sqrt6}\int_0^\infty(z^2-1)q'(z)e^{-(z^2+q(z)^2)/2}\,dz.
\]
The formulas for \(m_1,m_3\) follow from Gaussian integration by parts applied
to \(\mathbb E_Y\operatorname{sgn}(Y+q(z))=\operatorname{erf}(q(z)/\sqrt2)\).
The reflected diffuse cubic component has the opposite sign for \(g_n\),
which accounts for the minus sign in the third-chaos pair.

For completeness, one can justify convergence of the actual original-chaos
projections, rather than just convergence in distribution, as follows. Replace
the signs temporarily by a bounded smooth approximation with bounded
\(y\)-derivatives. Taylor expansion in one summand \(H_3(X_j)/\sqrt n\)
shows that its \(H_3(X_j)\) coefficient is
\(n^{-1/2}\mathbb E\partial_y f+O(n^{-3/2})\). Its \(H_1(X_j)\)
coefficient is \(O(n^{-3/2})\), and its \(H_2(X_j)\) coefficient is
\(O(n^{-1})\): these follow respectively from
\(\mathbb E[H_1H_3]=\mathbb E[H_1H_3^2]=0\) and
\(\mathbb E[H_2H_3]=0\). Expand independently in two or three coordinates
for the mixed terms. The sums of squared coefficients of all other total-degree
one or three terms tend to zero. The retained \(H_3(X_j)\) coefficients give
\(\mathbb E[Y f]\) in the limit by normal integration by parts, while the
pure \(Z\) coefficients give \(m_1,m_3\). Projection is a contraction in
\(L^2\), so the smooth approximation can then be removed. In this witness
\(q'(z)\ge511/200>0\); conditioning on \(Y_n\) gives a uniform small-band
bound for \(|Y_n+q(Z)|\), which makes the approximation uniform in \(n\).

The complete degree accounting, for fixed smooth approximation, is as follows.
The coefficient sizes are uniform in the indices; the final column includes
all coefficients of the stated type. Here i,j,k are distinct transverse
indices. The signs affect limits but not the orders.

| Original Hermite basis term | Coefficient size | Squared-energy order after summing |
|---|---:|---:|
| H1(Z) | tends to m1 | retained |
| H1(Xi) | O(n^(-3/2)) | O(n^(-2)) |
| H3(Z) | tends to m3 | retained |
| H2(Z) H1(Xi) | O(n^(-3/2)) | O(n^(-2)) |
| H1(Z) H2(Xi) | O(n^(-1)) | O(n^(-1)) |
| H1(Z) H1(Xi) H1(Xj) | O(n^(-3)) | O(n^(-4)) |
| H3(Xi) | n^(-1/2) mY + o(n^(-1/2)) | tends to mY^2 |
| H2(Xi) H1(Xj) | O(n^(-5/2)) | O(n^(-3)) |
| H1(Xi) H1(Xj) H1(Xk) | O(n^(-9/2)) | O(n^(-6)) |

There are no other basis types in total degrees one and three. For the
reflected sign g, the retained pure-Z limits remain m1,m3 and the transverse
cubic limit is -mY. This proves both cross-level pairing formulas.

## Integration enclosure

On each of 320 rational cells in \([0,5]\), the code integrates the even
Taylor terms through degree eight at the midpoint and bounds the order-ten
remainder over the whole cell with directed MPFR intervals. Thus it does not
assume numerical quadrature convergence. For the tail, because \(q(z)\ge az\)
on \(z\ge0\), put \(c_0=1+a^2\). Then
\[
 e^{-(z^2+q(z)^2)/2}\le e^{-c_0z^2/2},\quad
 J_0\le\frac{e^{-c_0L^2/2}}{c_0L},\quad
 J_2\le\frac{Le^{-c_0L^2/2}+J_0}{c_0},\quad
 J_4\le\frac{L^3e^{-c_0L^2/2}+3J_2}{c_0}.
\]
These bound all three omitted integrals. The MPFR precision is 256 bits;
`GR_MPFR_BITS` can raise it.

## What remains for an improvement

The target needs a uniform upper estimate over all Gaussian dimensions and
all bounded sign pairs, not only these reflected thresholds. The old fixed
profile transfer route has been independently checked in
`../../history/epsilon720_transfer_audit.json`: every one of the 32 retained
five-price whole-frame costs is already above \(40/71\) at coefficient .72.
That is a failure of this sufficient proof route, not a norm obstruction.
The inherited low-amplitude energy estimate also exceeds the target at zero;
the elementary high-amplitude tail estimate passes. New local profiles and
new source exclusions are therefore needed before the new matrices yield an
improved certified lower bound.

The neighboring `../joint/` package proves that baseline norm, separate
chaos-energy, phase, support first-moment, and finitely many Borell constraints
alone leave a formal relaxation point that blocks every improvement beyond
1.773. It identifies which missing joint sign geometry a new proof must use.

## A ceiling for every pure cubic coefficient

Run `python certify_cubic_family_ceiling.py` for a second exact replay.
At fixed c=40812698/10^8, the two rational endpoints

a_low=264042705/10^8 and a_high=264042707/10^8

have respectively negative and positive third-chaos pairing, enclosed as
approximately -6.00947574*10^(-10) and +6.15133889*10^(-10).
By continuity there is an exact intermediate a_star with zero third-chaos
pairing. The first moment is strictly increasing in a, because

\[
 \frac{d}{da}\mathbb E[Z\operatorname{erf}(q(Z)/\sqrt2)]
 =\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\mathbb E[Z^2e^{-q(Z)^2/2}]>0.
\]

Thus, for **every real b**, the same limiting sign construction gives

\[
 \sup_d\|P_1-bP_3\|_{\infty\to1}
 \ge0.563233523593909928766\ldots.
\]

Every reciprocal-norm certificate using a pure cubic operator therefore has
endpoint at most **1.775462500206215874**. This is a ceiling for this method,
not a Grothendieck upper bound. It still leaves the target1.775 possible;
adding a new chaos coefficient would be needed to get substantially farther.
The enclosing result, rational parameters, and endpoint integrations are all
in `cubic_family_ceiling.json`.
