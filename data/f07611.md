# Physical coupling constraints for the cubic middle region

These statements apply to actual odd Gaussian signs \(f,g\),
\(h=(f+g)/2\), \(k=(f-g)/2\), and \(P_1h=uZ\), with \(Z\)
one standard Gaussian coordinate and \(W\) the independent remaining
coordinates. They do not treat arbitrary moment vectors as realizable.
They yield valid source restrictions, but do not close the global
\(P_1-(18/25)P_3\) norm estimate.

## 1. Sharp support mass versus agreement amplitude

Put \(\nu=\sqrt{2/\pi}\), \(m=\mathbb Eh^2\). Since \(h\)
is a ternary function and \(|h|=h^2\), Gaussian rearrangement gives
\[
u=\mathbb E[Zh]\le\mathbb E[|Z|h^2]
\le2\phi(t),\qquad m=2\overline\Phi(t).
\]
Equality is attained by \(h=\operatorname{sgn}Z\,
\mathbf1_{\{|Z|>t\}}\), with a disjoint odd \(k\) on the
complement. Therefore for \(0<u\le\nu\),
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathbb Ek^2\le1-2\overline\Phi\left(
\sqrt{2\log(\nu/u)}\right).
\quad}
\tag{1}
\]
The endpoint \(u=0\) is interpreted by continuity. This is stronger
than the simple \(\mathbb Ek^2\le1-u^2\), but support alone
does not restrict the cubic projection sufficiently in the middle.

For conditional coefficients
\(h_1(W)=\mathbb E_Z[Zh]\), \(k_0(W)=\mathbb E_Zk\), let
\[
F(p)=\nu\exp\left[-\frac12
\Phi^{-1}\left(\frac{1+p}{2}\right)^2\right],\qquad0\le p\le1.
\]
The sharper pointwise physical restriction is
\[
|h_1(W)|\le F(|k_0(W)|).
\tag{2}
\]
For \(p=2\Phi(t)-1\), one has \(F'(p)=-t\) and
\(F''(p)=-1/F(p)<0\). Jensen's inequality therefore implies
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathbb E|k_0|\le
2\Phi\left(\sqrt{2\log(\nu/u)}\right)-1.
\quad}
\tag{3}
\]
This bounds the absolute conditional mean, not the support mass of
\(k\). They must not be confused. In particular the support-mass
gate bounds from the companion source-bridge proof apply to
\(\mathbb E|k_0|\) when their source is transverse.

## 2. The exact longitudinal variance profile of a full cubic source

Suppose \(r=\|P_3k\|_2>0\) and set \(Q=P_3k/r\). This is the
full cubic source, unlike \(P_3^Wk_0\). With normalized Hermites
\(H_j=\operatorname{He}_j/\sqrt{j!}\), its exact decomposition is
\[
Q(Z,W)=\sum_{j=0}^3H_j(Z)Q_{3-j}(W),
\qquad Q_{3-j}\in\mathcal H_{3-j}(W).
\]
Let \(w_j=\|Q_{3-j}\|_2^2\). Orthogonality gives
\(w_j\ge0\), \(\sum_jw_j=1\), and the physical conditional
second moment is
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathbb E_W[Q^2\mid Z=z]
=s_w(z):=w_0+w_1z^2+
\frac{w_2}{2}(z^2-1)^2+
\frac{w_3}{6}(z^3-3z)^2.
\quad}
\tag{4}
\]

If \(w_0=1\), the source is independent of \(Z\) exactly.
Constant conditional variance alone is a weaker fact: allowing
arbitrary conditional distributions with that variance would
incorrectly admit dependence on \(Z\), and lose the sharper
independent-source \(J_a\) price.

## 3. A valid mean-and-conditional-variance source bound

Use the now-proved \(\mathbb E|Q|\le\rho=849/1000\).
For \(a>0\), \(0\le\lambda<1\), put \(c=1-\lambda\) and
\[
\Psi_\lambda(A,s)=
\begin{cases}
A+c^2s/(4A),& c\sqrt s\le2A,\\
c\sqrt s,&c\sqrt s\ge2A,
\end{cases}\qquad A>0.
\]
At \(A=0\), use its limit \(c\sqrt s\); at \(\lambda=1\),
put \(\Psi_1(A,s)=A\). Then every actual sign pair satisfies
\[
\boxed{\quad
au+r\le\lambda\rho+
\mathbb E_Z\Psi_\lambda(a|Z|,s_w(Z)).
\quad}
\tag{5}
\]

To prove this, pointwise disjointness gives
\(aZh+Qk\le\max(a|Z|,|Q|)\), and \(\mathbb E Qk=r\).
For fixed \(A\), the function \(\Psi_\lambda(A,x)\) is a
concave majorant of
\(\max(A,\sqrt x)-\lambda\sqrt x\). Its first segment is the
line joining the value \(A\) at zero to the tangent at
\(x=4A^2/c^2\); the second segment is \(c\sqrt x\).
Conditional Jensen applied to \(x=Q^2\) proves (5).

Thus an entirely physical upper relaxation for the full cubic source
is obtained by maximizing the right side over the four-variable
simplex \(w\), then minimizing over \(a,\lambda\). Numerical
exploration indicates that this relaxation alone is too loose: it
does not retain the exact independence at \(w_0=1\). The theorem
is nonetheless useful for separating source profiles with appreciable
longitudinal weight from the nearly transverse profiles, which need
the independent \(J_a\) and Stein estimates.

## 4. Which longitudinal perturbation enters at first order

Let \(Q_0(W)\) be a normalized pure cubic source and consider
\(\mathcal F_a(Q)=\mathbb E\max(a|Z|,|Q|)\).
For a longitudinal perturbation
\(D_j=H_j(Z)R_{3-j}(W)\), differentiation at \(Q_0\) gives
\[
D\mathcal F_a(Q_0)[D_j]
=\mathbb E\bigl[
\operatorname{sgn}(Q_0)\mathbf1_{\{|Q_0|>a|Z|\}}D_j\bigr].
\]
The exceptional equality event is null by the independent continuous
Gaussian \(Z\); dominated convergence uses \(\mathbb E|D_j|<\infty\).
For \(j=1,3\) this derivative is zero by \(Z\)-parity.
For \(j=2\), with \(R_1\) a normalized linear source, it is exactly
\[
\boxed{\quad
D\mathcal F_a(Q_0)[H_2(Z)R_1(W)]
=-\frac{\sqrt2}{a}\mathbb E[
R_1Q_0\phi(Q_0/a)].
\quad}
\tag{6}
\]
Indeed \(\mathbb E[H_2(Z)\mathbf1_{\{|Z|<t\}}]
=-\sqrt2\,t\phi(t)\).

The worst linear angular direction is therefore governed by
\((\sqrt2/a)\|P_1^W[Q_0\phi(Q_0/a)]\|_2\).
Although \(P_1Q_0=0\), this nonlinear first projection need not
vanish. Setting it to zero based on chaos orthogonality would be
incorrect. For the optimal independent-source conditional mean
\(k_0^a=\operatorname{sgn}(Q_0)
\operatorname{erf}(|Q_0|/(\sqrt2a))\),
\[
\frac{d}{da}P_1^Wk_0^a
=-\frac2{a^2}P_1^W[Q_0\phi(Q_0/a)].
\tag{7}
\]
Thus the only first-order longitudinal obstruction is coupled to the
actual first projection of \(k_0\), whose squared norm carries a
negative price in the operator objective. This identifies a concrete
physical coupling that a stronger local certificate can retain.

Equations (1)--(7) are proved restrictions, not a proof that the
middle range is closed. A complete operator certificate must control
the nonlinear first projection in (6), or retain its negative first-
projection price jointly in the one-dimensional fiber comparison.
