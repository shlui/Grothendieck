# Independent tensor-shape audit

The constraint **κζ ≤ 36 is correct** for every normalized real Gaussian third-chaos variable. This is a necessary physical constraint, and does not require a new empirical or numerical assumption.

Let `f` be a symmetric real order-three Hilbert tensor with norm one, and use the standard Wiener normalization `E I_j(g)^2 = j! ||g||²`. Put `Q=I_3(f)/sqrt(6)`, `g=f⊗₂f`, `h=sym(f⊗₁f)`, `A=||g||²`, and `B=||h||²`. The Gaussian product formula gives

\[
Q^2=1+3I_2(g)+\tfrac32 I_4(h)+\tfrac16 I_6(\operatorname{sym}(f\otimes f)),
\qquad \Gamma=\tfrac13\|DQ\|^2=1+2I_2(g)+\tfrac12I_4(h).
\]

The squared norm of the last order-six tensor is `(2+18A)/20`. To see this, average over the twenty ways to choose which three of its six indices belong to the first copy of `f`. Two choices preserve the original grouping and have inner product one. The nine choices sharing one index and nine sharing two indices have inner product `A`. Hence orthogonality gives

\[
\kappa=\mathbb E Q^4-3=36A+54B,\qquad
V=\mathbb E(\Gamma-1)^2=8A+6B.
\]

For κ>0 the established shape coordinate satisfies `V=κ(1+ζ)/9`. Eliminating B gives `κζ=9V−κ=36A`. Flatten `f` as a matrix `F` with one index on the row and two on the column. Then `g=FFᵀ`, `tr(g)=||f||²=1`, and `g` is positive semidefinite. Its nonnegative eigenvalues therefore imply `A=tr(g²)≤tr(g)²=1`. Thus `κζ≤36`.

For κ=0 the inequality is automatic; no shape needs to be defined by division. The independently established range ζ∈[2/5,1] continues to apply. In particular κ≥60 requires ζ≤36/κ, and κ=90 permits only ζ=2/5. The univariate normalized Hermite cubic attains κ=90, V=14, A=B=1, giving a useful normalization check.

For a cell κ=k₀+(k₁−k₀)t and ζ=z₀+(z₁−z₀)s, use the McCormick variable w=t s. The added relaxation constraint is exactly affine:

\[
k_0z_0+(k_1-k_0)z_0t+k_0(z_1-z_0)s
 +(k_1-k_0)(z_1-z_0)w\le36.
\]

Every physical point has a lift satisfying this inequality. Therefore intersecting the existing lifted polytope with it is a valid outer relaxation. A generic rectangle corner check that simply deletes infeasible corners is insufficient: the new curved boundary may contain a maximizer. Complete lifted vertex pricing, or another proved enclosure of the hyperbolic boundary, is required.
