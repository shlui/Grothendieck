## A rigorous perturbation consequence of the frozen inputs

Write
\[
a=\frac{2403891349687}{2000000000000},\quad
b=\frac{12706580435619}{10000000000000},\quad
\varepsilon_0=\frac{699}{1000},\quad U=\frac{1000}{1773}.
\]
The retained inputs are the dimension-uniform norm cap \(N(T_{\varepsilon_0})\le U\) and the mixed-energy inequality
\(a\|P_1v\|_2^2+b\|P_3v\|_2^2\le1\) for bounded \(v\).

For a scalar pair use \(h=(f+g)/2,k=(f-g)/2\) and put
\(x=\|P_1h\|_2^2,y=\|P_1k\|_2^2,s=\|P_3h\|_2^2,t=\|P_3k\|_2^2\).
Then
\[
a(x+y)+b(s+t)\le1,\qquad
q_0=x-y+\varepsilon_0(t-s)\le U.
\]
Set \(r=t-s\). Positivity gives
\[
a q_0+(b-a\varepsilon_0)r\le1.
\]
Therefore, for \(0\le\delta\le(b-a\varepsilon_0)/a\),
\[
q_{\varepsilon_0+\delta}=q_0+\delta r
\le U+\delta\frac{1-aU}{b-a\varepsilon_0}.
\]
Taking the supremum over all bounded pairs proves
\[
N(T_{\varepsilon_0+\delta})
\le U+0.7481652530949529\ldots\,\delta.
\]
For \(r=s-t\) the same argument yields
\[
N(T_{\varepsilon_0-\delta})
\le U+0.152587123573534\ldots\,\delta
\]
when \(0\le\delta\le(b+a\varepsilon_0)/a\).

Both slopes improve on the triangle slope \(1/b=.786994\ldots\) supplied by the same energy theorem. Both are positive. Thus these consequences certify nearby operators with **weaker** reciprocal lower bounds, not an improvement above 1.773. For example, at \(\varepsilon=.699001\) the new bound gives only 1.7729976481.

The positive-shift inequality is sharp within these aggregate necessary constraints: the nonnegative moment point
\[
x=U-\varepsilon_0\eta,\quad y=s=0,\quad t=\eta,\quad
\eta=\frac{1-aU}{b-a\varepsilon_0}

saturates the energy constraint and has \(q_0=U\). It is not claimed to be realizable by Gaussian sign functions. It shows why these aggregate constraints alone cannot remove the positive perturbation cost.

## Conditional consequence of a new uniform cubic \(L_1\) bound

Suppose a new certificate proves
\[
\sup_{\|Q\|_2=1,\ Q\in\mathcal H_3}\mathbb E|Q|\le\rho
\]
in every dimension. This is equivalent by Hilbert-space duality to
\(\|P_3v\|_2\le\rho\) for \(\|v\|_\infty\le1\). Hence the half-sum energies satisfy \(s+t\le\rho^2\).

With \(u^2=x\), discard the nonpositive \(y,s\) terms to obtain
\[
q_\varepsilon(u)\le
\min\left\{
u^2+\varepsilon\rho^2,\
\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon a}{b}\right)u^2+\frac{\varepsilon}{b}
\right\}.
\]
Convexly combining the two energy theorems does not improve this minimum. For weight \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), the resulting fraction is
\[
u^2+\varepsilon
\frac{1-\lambda a u^2}
{\rho^{-2}-\lambda(\rho^{-2}-b)}.
\]
Its derivative has constant sign in \(\lambda\), so the minimum occurs at an endpoint. The bounds switch at
\(u^2=(1-b\rho^2)/a\).
