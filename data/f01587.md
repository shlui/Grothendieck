# Independent reduced-class and endpoint-scope audit

Audit date: 2026-10-01. Reviewed the current `ratio_maps.md`, `universal_reduced_sequence.md`, `analytic_reflection_seed.md`, the historical transfer review, the joint-cap limitation, and the directed `.72` lower-witness implementation. Result: the stated matrix reductions and their norm factors are correct. They preserve the available endpoint; they do not create a larger endpoint.

## Weighted copies and the absolute doubly stochastic class

If `P` has at most one nonzero per row and `R` at most one nonzero per column, the aggregate of each original scalar group lies in its total-absolute-weight interval. Every interval vertex is attainable. The vector aggregate lies in the corresponding Euclidean ball, and every original unit vector can be copied with signs to attain its boundary. Separate left/right orthogonal coordinates complete ball vectors to unit length. Therefore

\[
C(PAR)=C(D_p A D_r),\qquad Q(PAR)=Q(D_p A D_r).
\]

Constant positive totals `p_i=c`, `r_j=d` give common norm scaling `cd`, hence exact ratio invariance. The notes correctly distinguish this theorem from a classification of all possible ratio-only maps. The stronger universal common-norm-scaling classification follows from testing rank-one matrices and the disjoint-support characterization of real linear `l1` isometries.

The weighted-CHSH converse is valid for original dimensions at least two. Its scalar formula and Cauchy–Schwarz quantum bound give a strict ratio loss whenever either pair of copying totals differs. The one-sided-dimension-one exception is correctly retained.

For a nonzero integer matrix without zero rows/columns, row masses `r_i` and column masses `c_j` are positive integers. Cloning each row `r_i` times and each column `c_j` times with coefficient `Z_ij/(r_i c_j)` produces a square matrix of order `L=sum |Z_ij|`. Its absolute row and column sums are one. Both norms equal the original norms exactly. This is not ordinary diagonal scaling: the copies are essential.

For arbitrary real input, rational density and the total-entry-error Lipschitz bound give a supremum reduction. The notes correctly avoid asserting finite integer clone counts for irrational masses. Computable dyadic approximations can be selected by certified intervals, so an undecidable exact rounding tie is unnecessary.

## Simultaneous symmetry and balancing

The symmetric dilation

\[
K(B)=\begin{pmatrix}0&B\\B^T&0\end{pmatrix}
\]

has `C(K)=2 C(B)` and `Q(K)=2 Q(B)`. Its two bilinear off-diagonal terms use disjoint left/right lists; they can attain their maxima simultaneously. Quadratic versus bilinear objectives must remain distinguished, especially for the skew dilation.

The matrix

\[
J_0=\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad H=J_0\otimes K(B)
\]

is an exact signed two-copy map with common copying mass `sqrt(2)` on each side. Hence

\[
C(H)=4C(B),\qquad Q(H)=4Q(B).
\]

If `|B|` is doubly stochastic, `H` is symmetric, zero-diagonal, has zero row and column sums, and `|H|` is doubly stochastic. All four properties hold simultaneously. They do not imply an improvement of the ratio or a bound on the dimension needed for a near-optimal ratio.

## Reflection representation and the zero-weight distinction

For a symmetric zero-diagonal contraction `H` of order `N`, its functional-calculus square root `R=(I-H^2)^(1/2)` commutes with `H`. Thus

\[
U=\begin{pmatrix}H&R\\R&-H\end{pmatrix},\quad
P=(I-U)/2
\]

satisfy `U^T=U`, `U^2=I`, `P^T=P=P^2`, `rank(P)=N`, and every diagonal entry of `P` is one half. With

\[
D=\operatorname{diag}((2/N)I_N,0_N),
\]

the exact identity is

\[
D-2D^{1/2}PD^{1/2}=(2/N)H\oplus0_N.
\]

Its ratio is unchanged. The trace of `D` is two and all its nonzero diagonal weights are equal. If `H 1=0`, then `R 1=1`, `U 1=1`, and `P 1=0`. The resulting matrix has zero sums. If `|H|` is doubly stochastic, its active absolute row sums are `2/N`, matching its active diagonal weights; its auxiliary rows vanish.

This reflection matrix is not an ordinary absolute doubly stochastic matrix on its full padded support. The absolute doubly stochastic representative is `H`; the reflection representative is a scaled zero-padding of it. The notes explicitly make this distinction.

In contrast, strictly positive uniform full-support weights yield a scalar multiple of an orthogonal matrix. For any real orthogonal matrix `U` of order `N`, `Q(U)=N`, while Gaussian hyperplane rounding gives

\[
C(U)\ge(2/\pi)\sum_{ij}U_{ij}\arcsin U_{ij}
\ge(2/\pi)\sum_{ij}U_{ij}^2=(2/\pi)N.
\]

Thus its ratio is at most `pi/2`. Zero auxiliary weights are essential to the universal construction. They cannot be silently replaced by a positive common weight.

The analytic folded-cell reflection seed is also correct: the weighted compression `K=D_p^(-1/2) A D_p^(-1/2)` has spectral norm at most the original operator's `M`; its four-block diagonal `D0=(1/2)diag(p,p,p,p)` has trace two; the normalized contraction is exactly `(1/M) J0 tensor [[0,K],[K,0]]`; the final padded matrix is `H/(2M)`, with norm factor `2/M` from the original `A`. This is a separate weighted construction, not the absolute-mass cloning operation.

## Search barriers

Signed Birkhoff decomposition is valid by first decomposing `|B|` and then restoring the fixed entry signs. There is no entry cancellation in that representation. Fixing one signed permutation to the identity by signed left multiplication is a gauge on this class, not an ordinary orthogonal change of basis.

Disconnected bipartite support splits the two norms additively. Each absolute doubly stochastic component is square; the ratio is a classical-norm-weighted average. Selecting its best component can increase the ratio, so this selection should not be described as exact ratio preservation.

The no-articulation argument is valid: after removing a row vertex, each remaining component has positive integer column-minus-row count equal to the mass it receives from that removed row. The total mass is one, so there is only one remaining component. The column argument is identical. An all-positive cycle-sign pattern is removable by vertex signs and has ratio one. A relevant connected support therefore needs a negative-product cycle.

A mixture of two signed permutation matrices has bipartite degree at most two. Its components are edges or even cycles. Edges have ratio one, four-edge cycles at most `sqrt(2)`, and longer frustrated cycles with `r>=6` edges have `C >= W(1-2/r)` and `Q <= W`, hence ratio at most `r/(r-2)<=3/2`. Thus two signed matching terms cannot reach the proposed endpoint. Three terms are necessary, not proved sufficient.

## Accepted baseline and fresh candidate scope

The retained project baseline is `1.773` under its accepted dimension-uniform `.699` cubic cap and 55-cover premises. The currently recovered reports describe that proof and its inherited hash-bound integrations. This audit does not regenerate the missing full earlier archive and does not refute or replace the accepted premise. The later Song–Yue endpoint `1.7712846852087007...` is a separately theorem-supported, weaker endpoint. Exact cloning and reflection preserve either endpoint, and cannot amplify them.

The new `.72` implementation is a directed scalar norm **lower** witness for `P1-.72 P3`. It gives a reciprocal-norm method ceiling near `1.775436151034707...`, above the target `1.775`. It therefore does not rule out that target, and it also does not certify it. No global all-sign scalar norm upper bound for `.72` has been approved.

The old `.699` fixed-operator ceiling, and the failure of old sufficient `.72` transfer costs, are different statements. The former is an actual scalar lower witness against the unchanged old operator; the latter means a particular sufficient proof route fails, not that the actual new operator norm is too large.

The joint-cap formal Gram obstruction is correctly labeled as a relaxation point rather than an actual Gaussian sign pair. It limits only the listed black-box norm caps, energy/phase constraints, and finite lists of noise inequalities. It does not survive every known nonlinear conditional-fiber constraint, does not prove a ceiling for actual matrices, and does not bound the unrestricted real Grothendieck constant. The notes retain these distinctions.

One minor clarity recommendation: explicitly define `H3(x)=(x^3-3x)/sqrt(6)` in the new `.72` witness definition. The integrals and spectral CLT in the code use the orthonormal third Hermite. Leaving the symbol undefined could make a reader instead use the unnormalized probabilists' polynomial, whose limiting variance would be six.

No substantive correction to the reviewed reductions is required. The intended final claims should retain `1.773` with its accepted historical premise scope, identify the lossless reduced search class, state the full-support uniform-weight barrier, and leave `.72` as an uncertified candidate.
