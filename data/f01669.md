# A lossless reduced class and a simultaneous reflection representation

This note uses the bilinear real Grothendieck ratio
\(G(A)=Q(A)/C(A)\) of a nonzero finite real matrix. The definitions and
complete classification of universal *common norm scaling* are in
`ratio_maps.md`. A general orthogonal change of coordinates is not among
those transformations.

## 1. Absolute doubly stochastic matrices are universal

Let \(Z\) be a nonzero integer matrix without zero rows or columns. Put

\[
r_i=\sum_j|Z_{ij}|,\qquad c_j=\sum_i|Z_{ij}|,
\qquad L=\sum_i r_i=\sum_jc_j.
\]

Clone row \(i\) exactly \(r_i\) times and column \(j\) exactly
\(c_j\) times, and define the square matrix of order \(L\)

\[
B_{(i,a),(j,b)}=\frac{Z_{ij}}{r_i c_j}.
\tag{1}
\]

Each original row has total absolute copying weight one, as does each
original column. Consequently

\[
C(B)=C(Z),\qquad Q(B)=Q(Z),\qquad G(B)=G(Z).
\tag{2}
\]

For a cloned row the sum of absolute entries is
\(\sum_j c_j|Z_{ij}|/(r_ic_j)=1\); the column calculation is the
same. Thus \(|B|\) is doubly stochastic. Rational input is covered by
first clearing a common denominator. The preliminary common scaling
preserves the ratio.

For arbitrary real matrices this is a supremum reduction, rather than
an assertion that arbitrary irrational row masses have integer clone
counts. Indeed, for both norms,

\[
|C(A)-C(A')|\le\sum_{ij}|A_{ij}-A'_{ij}|,
\qquad
|Q(A)-Q(A')|\le\sum_{ij}|A_{ij}-A'_{ij}|.
\tag{3}
\]

The ratio is continuous at every nonzero matrix, and rational matrices
are dense. Therefore

\[
G_{\mathbb R}=\sup_{N\ge1}\ \sup_{|B|\mathbf1=|B|^T\mathbf1=\mathbf1}
G(B).
\tag{4}
\]

Diagonal rescaling \(D_r^{-1}AD_c^{-1}\) by itself does not implement
this reduction. The copies in (1) are essential.

## 2. Symmetry, zero diagonal and zero sums can hold simultaneously

For an absolute doubly stochastic \(B\) of order \(L\), define

\[
K(B)=\begin{pmatrix}0&B\\B^T&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad J_0=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad H(B)=J_0\otimes K(B).
\tag{5}
\]

The matrix \(H(B)\), of order \(N=4L\), is symmetric, has zero
diagonal, has zero row and column sums, and is again absolute doubly
stochastic. Its norms satisfy

\[
C(K(B))=2C(B),\quad Q(K(B))=2Q(B),
\qquad C(H(B))=4C(B),\quad Q(H(B))=4Q(B).
\tag{6}
\]

The two terms of the block dilation use disjoint scalar/vector lists,
so each can optimize independently. The factor two for \(J_0\) is an
exact weighted copying transformation: use the two copies with
coefficients \(1/\sqrt2,-1/\sqrt2\) on each side. Their common
absolute copying mass is \(\sqrt2\).

It follows that the matrices in (5), over all rational inputs and
orders, give a simultaneous universal class. This does not assert that
every matrix already in that class is of form (5), or that fixing a
particular order retains the full constant.

The analogous skew block uses
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&B\\-B^T&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
and has the same norm factors. Its bilinear objective is not zero.

## 3. Uniform weights on the retained support suffice for a reflection

Since \(|H|\) is doubly stochastic,
\(\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le
\sqrt{\|H\|_1\|H\|_\infty}=1\).
For any symmetric zero-diagonal contraction \(H\) of order \(N\), put

\[
R=(I-H^2)^{1/2},\qquad
U=\begin{pmatrix}H&R\\R&-H\end{pmatrix},\qquad
P=\frac{I-U}{2}.
\tag{7}
\]

The square root commutes with \(H\). Thus \(U=U^T\),
\(U^2=I\), and its diagonal is zero. Hence \(P=P^T=P^2\) and
\(P_{aa}=1/2\) for every coordinate. Define

\[
D=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac2N I_N,0_N\right).
\]

Then \(\operatorname{tr}D=2\), and

\[
\boxed{\quad
W=D-2D^{1/2}PD^{1/2}
  =D^{1/2}UD^{1/2}
  =\frac2N H\oplus0_N.
\quad}
\tag{8}
\]

In particular \(G(W)=G(H)\), with each norm scaled by \(2/N\).
For (5), zero sums, symmetry and zero diagonal are retained. Every
nonzero diagonal weight of \(D\) is equal. The zero auxiliary weights
are indispensable to this universal construction. The meaningful
absolute-doubly-stochastic representative is \(H\); the reflection
representative includes zero padding and an overall scale.

One cannot replace (7) by the apparently simpler expression
\(P'=SPS^T+(I-SS^T)/2\) for a splitting isometry \(S\): that
matrix has eigenvalue \(1/2\) on the new orthogonal complement and is
not a projection. The contraction dilation in (7) avoids this error.

## 4. A genuinely smaller but insufficient class

If instead every weight is the same positive value, \(D=dI_N\), then
the reflection matrix has the form \(W=dU\), where \(U\) is
orthogonal. This imposes a real restriction:

\[
Q(dU)=dN,\qquad C(dU)\ge\frac2\pi dN,
\qquad G(dU)\le\frac\pi2.
\tag{9}
\]

The spectral bound gives \(Q(dU)\le dN\); use standard basis vectors
on one side and the columns of \(U\) on the other for equality.
For the scalar bound, apply Gaussian sign rounding to that assignment.
Its expected objective is
\(d(2/\pi)\sum_{ij}U_{ij}\arcsin U_{ij}\), at least
\(d(2/\pi)\sum_{ij}U_{ij}^2=d(2/\pi)N\).
The inequality follows from \(x\arcsin x\ge x^2\) for
\(-1\le x\le1\).

Thus ordinary orthogonal matrices or strictly uniform full-support
weighted reflections cannot supply ratios near \(1.775\). Equal
weights on the nonzero support, together with zero-weight dilation
coordinates, remain universal by (8); these two classes must not be
confused.

## 5. Deterministic reduced sequences preserve a proved endpoint

Let \(A_n\) be any explicitly specified finite real sequence, with
\(C(A_n)\ge h>0\) and a certified lower bound
\(\liminf G(A_n)\ge \ell\). Let its size be \(m_n\)-by-\(k_n\).
Choose dyadic rational \(\widehat A_n\) with

\[
\sum_{ij}|\widehat A_{n,ij}-A_{n,ij}|\le\eta_n,
\qquad \eta_n\downarrow0.
\tag{10}
\]

For example use nearest dyadic rounding with precision \(s_n\)
chosen so \(m_nk_n2^{-s_n-1}\le\eta_n\), with an explicit tie
rule. Since the analytic entries are computable, one can instead select
the first dyadic satisfying a rigorously certified error interval;
that choice does not require deciding equality at a rounding tie.

Clear the common denominator, delete zero rows and columns, apply
(1), then (5). The resulting sequence \(H_n\) consists of rational
symmetric, zero-diagonal, zero-sum, absolute doubly stochastic matrices
and satisfies exactly

\[
G(H_n)=G(\widehat A_n).
\]

Equation (3) implies

\[
G(\widehat A_n)\ge
\frac{Q(A_n)-\eta_n}{C(A_n)+\eta_n}.
\tag{11}
\]

Consequently \(\liminf G(H_n)\ge\ell\), and if the original ratios
converge then so do the reduced ratios to the same limit. Equation (8)
gives a simultaneous weighted-reflection sequence with exactly those
ratios. All steps are finite and deterministic, although the clone
orders may be enormous. No improved endpoint follows from an exact
ratio-preserving transformation alone.

This transfers both the retained \(1.773\) sequence, conditional on its
retained global norm premises, and the newly audited Song--Yue sequence
with theorem-supported endpoint \(1.7712846852087007\ldots\).
It neither independently verifies the old hash-bound premise nor
improves either lower bound.

## 6. A useful finite search parameterization

By a signed Birkhoff decomposition every absolute doubly stochastic
matrix is a convex combination
\(B=\sum_{a=1}^t\theta_a S_a\) of signed permutation matrices,
with \(\theta_a\ge0\), \(\sum_a\theta_a=1\), and signs chosen
from the matrix's support, so no entry cancellations are needed. This
follows by decomposing \(|B|\) into permutations and restoring the
fixed entry signs. At fixed order the continuous parameters can
therefore be taken as simplex weights, with signed permutations as
discrete data. Left multiplication by \(S_1^T\) fixes one of the
permutations to \(I\); common signed conjugations remain a gauge.

For a disconnected support the two norms add over components, and
the ratio is a \(C\)-weighted average of their ratios. Each component
of an absolute doubly stochastic matrix is square and retains that
property. At least one component has ratio at least the original.
Thus a high-ratio search may concentrate on connected signed supports.
This last component selection preserves or increases the ratio rather
than preserving it identically.

Every connected support component is either a single edge or has no
articulation vertex. To prove this, remove a row vertex \(v\). For a
remaining component with row set \(R_a\) and column set \(C_a\),
mass conservation gives
\( |C_a|-|R_a|=\sum_{j\in C_a}|B_{vj}|>0\).
It is therefore a positive integer. Summing over all components gives
the total row mass one, so there can be only one remaining component.
The same argument applies on deleting a column vertex. Furthermore,
if every support cycle has positive sign product, vertex sign changes
make every matrix entry nonnegative, and both norms equal its total
mass. A relevant connected support must therefore have a cycle with
negative sign product.

A convex combination of only two signed permutations cannot approach
the target: its bipartite support has degree at most two and hence is
a union of isolated edges and even cycles. Isolated edges have ratio
one. A four-edge cycle has at most two columns and ratio at most
\(\sqrt2\): if the two column vectors make an angle \(2\theta\),
each row contribution is bounded by
\(|a+b|\cos\theta+|a-b|\sin\theta\); summing gives at most
\(C(\cos\theta+\sin\theta)\le\sqrt2 C\).
For a frustrated cycle with \(r\ge6\) edges and total absolute
mass \(W\), signs can satisfy all edges except the lightest one, so
\(C\ge W(1-2/r)\), while \(Q\le W\).
Its ratio is at most \(r/(r-2)\le3/2\).
Thus the entire two-permutation combination has ratio at most
\(3/2\). At least three signed matching terms are needed for a
ratio above that ceiling. This is only a necessary condition, not a
claim that three terms suffice.

These restrictions reduce redundant search coordinates. They do not
bound the order required, remove the finite sign optimization, or
certify a coefficient perturbation without a new scalar-norm bound.
