# The stronger linear tensor-shape constraint

For every variance-one real Gaussian third-chaos Q, its physical cumulant and shape satisfy

\[
\boxed{\quad4\kappa\zeta-\kappa\le54.\quad}
\]

This follows from a stronger tensor inequality than the squared estimate in `STRONGER_TENSOR_SHAPE_CONSTRAINT.md`. Retain `A=||f⊗₂f||²`, `B=||sym(f⊗₁f)||²`, `||f||=1`, and the exact identities `κ=36A+54B`, `κζ=36A`.

Let λ be the largest eigenvalue of the contraction matrix `g=f⊗₂f`. Because g is positive semidefinite with trace one,

\[
A=\sum_i\lambda_i^2\le\lambda^2+(1-\lambda)^2.
\]

If A>1/2, then λ≥A>1/2. For a unit largest-eigenvector v, the two commuting tensor-coordinate projections imposing v give `p=Σ_a f(v,v,e_a)²≥2λ−1>0`. Evaluating the symmetric order-four tensor in four v slots gives the value p. Hence

\[
B\ge p^2\ge(2\lambda-1)^2
 \ge2A-1.
\]

If A≤1/2, B≥0 suffices. Therefore **B≥(2A−1)₊**. For A>1/2 this yields `κ≥36A+54(2A−1)=144A−54=4κζ−54`. For A≤1/2 the same linear conclusion follows from `κ≥36A` and `36A≤18`. No negative lower bound is squared in this proof. Equality occurs at the univariate normalized Hermite cubic, where κ=90, ζ=2/5, A=B=1.

With the established `0≤κ≤90`, `2/5≤ζ≤1`, the new inequality already implies `κζ≤36` and the quadratic constraint previously derived. In particular,

\[
\zeta\le\min\left(1,\frac14+\frac{27}{2\kappa}\right),
\]

with no division needed at κ=0. The upper shape bound equals one at κ=18, .475 at κ=60, and .4 at κ=90. All these constraints describe the physical cubic source only and do not depend on an operator coefficient or a selected local profile.

## Exact maximum of the accepted price families

Write the accepted R3 moment price as

\[
F(k,z)=C+L_0 k+L_1kz+B_*k^2
 +A_*[K_0-K_1(z-2/5)]k^{3/2}
 +\tfrac94B_*(kz)^{3/2},
\]

where `A_*≥0`, `B_*≥0`, `K₀=194/√10`, `K₁=633/9`. These starred price coefficients differ from the physical tensor quantities A,B above. Fixed-z convexity and fixed-k convexity hold as previously proved. On the new curved boundary `z=1/4+27/(2k)` the price becomes

\[
C'+L'k+B_*k^2
 +A_*[K_0+(3/20)K_1]k^{3/2}
 -\tfrac{27}{2}A_*K_1k^{1/2}
 +\tfrac94B_*\left(\tfrac{k}{4}+\tfrac{27}{2}\right)^{3/2}.
\]

Every nonlinear term is convex: coefficients of k², k^(3/2), and the positive affine power 3/2 are nonnegative, while the square-root coefficient is nonpositive. Arbitrary linear terms cause no change. The B0 source prices and the accepted native/L1 scalar prices have the same form with `B_*=0` and a nonnegative sixth-moment coefficient `A_*`; their centering/variance additions are constant or linear in k,kz. Thus the same curved-boundary proof applies to all these guarded price families.

As in the earlier boundary argument, at fixed k the maximum lies on z₋ or `min(z₊,1/4+27/(2k))`. Convexity along each horizontal or curved interval gives the complete rational candidate set:

- feasible rectangle corners satisfying `k(4z−1)≤54`;
- vertical intersections `(k,(k+54)/(4k))` lying in the rectangle, omitting division at k=0;
- horizontal intersections `(54/(4z−1),z)` lying in the rectangle.

Because z≥2/5, the last denominator is strictly positive. If `k₋(4z₋−1)>54`, the rectangle is physically empty. These candidates cover every boundary transition, including k=18 when z₊=1. All numerical candidate prices still need directed arithmetic and accepted local/profile bindings. A global norm theorem additionally requires complete source/amplitude coverage. The note supplies a theorem and a pricing rule, not a new numerical norm cap.
