# A negative quintic test direction needs no eighth moment

This observation concerns the scalar Stein optimization only. It does not establish a new scalar price by itself.

For a normalized cubic chaos, write \(U=\|DQ\|^2/3-1\ge-1\) and \(V=\mathbb E U^2\). For a target \(h\) with Gaussian Stein derivative \(f_h'\), the valid clipped Young bound is
\[
 \mathbb E h(Q)\le\mathbb E h(Z)+
 \mathbb E[\phi'(Q)-Q\phi(Q)+\Psi_\eta(f_h'(Q)-\phi'(Q))]
 +\eta V/2,
\]
where \(\Psi_\eta(t)=t^2/(2\eta)\) for \(t\le\eta\), and \(t-\eta/2\) otherwise.

The current rational test basis has polynomial directions of degree at most four. Add an odd quintic direction
\[
 \phi(x)=\phi_0(x)+v_4x^5/5,\qquad v_4<0,
\]
where \(\phi_0(x)=O(x^4)\), and assume \(f_h'(x)=O(x^3)\) on the positive tail (the smoothed source targets and finite affine-tail native targets satisfy stronger bounds). Then the residual \(f_h'(x)-\phi'(x)\) is eventually positive and exceeds \(\eta\). On that tail,
\[
 \phi'(x)-x\phi(x)+\Psi_\eta(f_h'(x)-\phi'(x))
 =f_h'(x)-x\phi(x)-\eta/2,
\]
whose leading term is \(-v_4x^6/5\). Thus a degree-six moment majorant is compatible with this quintic direction. A necessary leading-coefficient gate is \(q_6\ge-v_4/5\); a strict reserve and a complete tail polynomial check are sufficient once the lower-order terms and derivative bounds are included.

Using the symmetric quadratic Young penalty on the entire tail would instead create an \(x^8\) term. The clipped inequality, justified by \(U\ge-1\), is essential. The expectation of \(Q\phi(Q)\) uses only the sixth moment, so the integration-by-parts step itself also needs no eighth moment.

This direction can exploit the exact identity
\[
 \mathbb E[UQ^4]=\mathbb E Q^6/5-\mathbb E Q^4,
\]
which follows by taking \(g(x)=x^5\) in the fixed-chaos Stein identity. It is not represented by the existing polynomial-degree-four test basis. Rational Chebyshev terms approach constants at infinity and cannot supply the missing polynomial quintic tail.

For comparison, centering \(U\) by its \(Q^2-1\) regression does not add a direction to the current basis. With
\(\alpha=\kappa/[3(\kappa+2)]\), replacing \(U\) by \(U-\alpha(Q^2-1)\) gives the same optimized quadratic Young bound after shifting \(\phi'\) by \(\eta\alpha(x^2-1)\), already represented by the linear and cubic test terms.
