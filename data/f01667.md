# An explicit balanced weighted-reflection sequence without solving an SDP

Let \(A_n=A_n^T\) be a finite matrix obtained by compressing a
selfadjoint Gaussian operator to antipodally folded cells. Write its
positive folded cell masses as \(p_n\), with \(\sum_i p_{n,i}=1\).
Suppose the operator's ordinary \(L^2\) norm is bounded by the explicit
constant \(M>0\). Then

\[
K_n=D_{p_n}^{-1/2}A_nD_{p_n}^{-1/2},
\qquad \|K_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le M.
\tag{1}
\]

Indeed \(K_n\) is its compression in the orthonormal folded-cell
basis. This is an operator-norm fact and does not require the optimal
finite vector value \(Q(A_n)\) or an optimal SDP dual.

If \(A_n\) has order \(m_n\), set

\[
H_n=J_0\otimes
\begin{pmatrix}0&A_n\\A_n&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
J_0=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
D_n^0=\frac12\operatorname{diag}(p_n,p_n,p_n,p_n).
\tag{2}
\]

Thus \(H_n\) is symmetric, zero-diagonal and has zero row and column
sums. The four blocks in \(D_n^0\) follow the same ordering as (2),
and \(\operatorname{tr}D_n^0=2\). Define

\[
L_n=(D_n^0)^{-1/2}\frac{H_n}{2M}(D_n^0)^{-1/2}
 =\frac1M J_0\otimes
  \begin{pmatrix}0&K_n\\K_n&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3}
\]

It is a symmetric zero-diagonal contraction. Its explicit symmetric
orthogonal dilation and projection are

\[
U_n=\begin{pmatrix}
L_n&(I-L_n^2)^{1/2}\\
(I-L_n^2)^{1/2}&-L_n
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad P_n=\frac{I-U_n}{2}.
\tag{4}
\]

Here \(P_n^2=P_n=P_n^T\), every diagonal entry is \(1/2\), and
\(\operatorname{rank}P_n=4m_n\). With
\(D_n=\operatorname{diag}(D_n^0,0_{4m_n})\), the required sequence is

\[
\boxed{\quad
W_n=D_n-2D_n^{1/2}P_nD_n^{1/2}
 =\frac{H_n}{2M}\oplus0_{4m_n}.
\quad}
\tag{5}
\]

It has trace-normalized diagonal weights \(\operatorname{tr}D_n=2\)
and is simultaneously symmetric, balanced and zero-diagonal. The
weighted-reflection representation is an exact identity, including
the zero auxiliary coordinates. It is fully specified by matrix square
roots and analytic cell moments.

The exact norm factors are

\[
C(H_n)=4C(A_n),\quad Q(H_n)=4Q(A_n),
\qquad C(W_n)=\frac2M C(A_n),\quad
Q(W_n)=\frac2M Q(A_n).
\tag{6}
\]

Consequently \(G(W_n)=G(A_n)\) for every \(n\). If the original
sequence has \(Q(A_n)\to M\) and \(C(A_n)\le c\), then
\(Q(W_n)\to2\), \(C(W_n)\le2c/M\), and
\(\liminf G(W_n)\ge M/c\).
