# A new uniform cubic-chaos energy bound

Let \(Q=I_3(f)\) be a real third Gaussian chaos, in any finite dimension, with
\(\mathbb E Q^2=1\). The newly replayed scalar certificates imply
\[
 \boxed{\mathbb E|Q|<\frac{849}{1000}=.849,\qquad
        \|P_3\|_{\infty\to2}^2\le\frac{720801}{1000000}.}     \tag{1}
\]
The scalar certificates in this note and the sharp 633 coupled-cumulant
theorem used by them have both been freshly reconstructed and checked. The
included proof chain consists of exact Wick identities, a new 84-rectangle
tensor comparison, and the eight complete-halfline Stein regressions below.
The original retained 99-rectangle data were not used. Independent checkers
also passed the new tensor comparison and all eight scalar regressions.

This is an energy lemma. It is not by itself a complete Grothendieck operator
norm bound or a new numerical lower endpoint for \(G_{\mathbb R}\).

## Included coupled-moment theorem and its provenance

Put
\[
 \kappa=\mathbb E Q^4-3,\quad
 \kappa_6=\mathbb E Q^6-15\mathbb E Q^4+30,\quad
 \tau_Q=\frac{\|DQ\|^2}{3},\quad U=\tau_Q-1,\quad V=\mathbb E U^2.
\]
The freshly checked coupled-moment theorem proves
\[
 0\le\kappa\le90,\qquad \frac{7\kappa}{45}\le V\le\frac{2\kappa}{9},
 \qquad
 \kappa_6\le K\kappa^{3/2}
    -633\sqrt\kappa\left(V-\frac{7\kappa}{45}\right),
 \quad K=\frac{194}{\sqrt{10}}.                               \tag{2}
\]
For \(\kappa>0\), an equivalent shape parameter is
\(z=9V/\kappa-1\in[2/5,1]\). This is merely a parametrization of the
coupled moment range; no probabilistic independence is assumed.

The formulas were recovered from
`recovered/Grothendieck Folder/Grothendieck_real_continuation_2026-09-30.tex`,
section *A stronger sixth-cumulant deficit*, theorem `thm:633`, equation
`eq:deficit633`. In the accompanying PDF the result is Section 3, Theorem 1,

Bernstein comparison with 2475 coefficients in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{10})\).
The included [fresh proof](633/PROOF.md) reconstructs the analytic reduction,

reconstruct the polynomials from the formulas and pass all coefficients,

[arXiv:2609.25462](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25462v1).

The fixed-chaos Stein identity used below is elementary Gaussian integration
by parts:
\[
 \mathbb E[Q g(Q)]=\mathbb E[\tau_Q g'(Q)]                     \tag{3}
\]
for the functions of polynomial growth appearing here. Its normalization is
\(\mathbb E\tau_Q=1\), because \(Q\) has degree three and variance one.

## Stein regression and the correct sign

Set \(c=\sqrt{2/\pi}\), and for \(x\ge0\) define the Mills ratio
\[
 R(x)=e^{x^2/2}\int_x^\infty e^{-t^2/2}\,dt.
\]
The odd extension of \(f_h(x)=-1+cR(x)\), for \(x\ge0\), solves
\(f_h'(x)-xf_h(x)=|x|-c\). Its derivative is even and is
\[
 f_h'(x)=-c(1-|x|R(|x|)).                                   \tag{4}
\]
For any odd continuously differentiable regression \(\phi\) with polynomial
growth, (3) gives
\[
 \mathbb E|Q|-c=-\mathbb E[U f_h'(Q)],\qquad
 \mathbb E[\phi'(Q)-Q\phi(Q)]=-\mathbb E[U\phi'(Q)].
\]
Young’s inequality, with \(\eta>0\), therefore yields
\[
 \mathbb E|Q|\le c+
 \mathbb E\!\left[\phi'(Q)-Q\phi(Q)
     +\frac{(f_h'(Q)-\phi'(Q))^2}{2\eta}\right]
    +\frac\eta2V.                                          \tag{5}
\]
This identity is the extra information absent from a scalar moment-only
relaxation. The accompanying exact moment-barrier example shows that moments
through degree six, even with (2), cannot alone imply (1).

## Exactly specified whole-line scalar rows

Every row freezes a positive rational \(\eta\), four rational coefficients
\(q_0,q_2,q_4,q_6\), with \(q_6>0\), and 17 rational regression coefficients.
For \(x\ge0\) the regression is
\[
 \phi(x)=v_0x+\frac{v_1x^2}{2}+\frac{v_2x^3}{3}
          +\frac{v_3x^4}{4}
  +\frac{x}{x+2}\sum_{j=0}^{12}v_{4+j}
               T_j\!\left(\frac{x-2}{x+2}\right),            \tag{6}
\]
and it is extended oddly. It vanishes at zero and has the same left and right
first derivative there. The exact row files, not floating discovery output,
specify all coefficients.

For each row a complete scalar certificate proves
\[
 \phi'(x)-x\phi(x)+\frac{(f_h'(x)-\phi'(x))^2}{2\eta}
 \le q_0+q_2x^2+q_4x^4+q_6x^6\qquad(x\in\mathbb R).          \tag{7}
\]
Fresh verification uses 512-bit outward Arb arithmetic and fourth-order Taylor
bounds on a complete adaptive cover of \([0,64]\). It encloses Mills’ ratio at
the interval endpoints and uses \(R'=xR-1\) to derive every required Taylor
coefficient. On \([64,\infty)\), the bounds
\(0\le1-xR(x)\le x^{-2}\), convexity of the square, and two exact rational
polynomial tests suffice. Translating the resulting polynomials by 64 gives
nonnegative coefficients and strictly positive constant terms, proving the
entire unbounded tail. Evenness covers negative arguments.

There is no finite-chaos sample, sampled maximum, omitted spatial tail, or
numerical extrapolation in the acceptance rules. Floating optimization was
used only to discover the rational row inputs.

## Coupled prices and complete cumulant cover

Apply (7) in (5) and substitute the sixth-moment bound from (2). The price is
affine in \(V\), so its maximum over the entire permitted shape interval occurs
at one of its endpoints. The two resulting prices are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 p_0(\kappa)&=c+q_0+q_2+(3+\kappa)q_4
  +[15+15\kappa+K\kappa^{3/2}]q_6+\frac{7\eta\kappa}{90},\\
 p_1(\kappa)&=c+q_0+q_2+(3+\kappa)q_4
  +[15+15\kappa+(K-633/15)\kappa^{3/2}]q_6
       +\frac{\eta\kappa}{9}.
 \end{aligned}                                               \tag{8}
\]
Since \(q_6>0\) and \(K-633/15>0\), both prices are convex in
\(\kappa\ge0\). Thus the maximum over a closed cumulant interval is bounded
by the four shape/cumulant corner prices. The endpoint \(\kappa=0\) is included
directly, without dividing by it.

The energy statement follows by duality:
\[
 \sup_{|f|\le1}\|P_3f\|_2
  =\sup_{\substack{Q\in\mathcal H_3\\\|Q\|_2=1}}\mathbb E|Q|.
\]
For the coefficient \(b=18/25=.72\), (1) gives
\[
 b\|P_3\|_{\infty\to2}^2\le
 \frac{18}{25}\frac{720801}{1000000}
       =\frac{6487209}{12500000}=.51897672
 <\frac{40}{71}.
\]
This closes the zero-first-amplitude energy obstruction and gives room to
extend that branch. Other amplitudes of the bilinear Grothendieck operator
still require their own valid global certificates.

More precisely, for arbitrary signs let \(h=(f+g)/2\), \(k=(f-g)/2\) and
\(u=\|P_1h\|_2\). The operator’s bilinear objective is the difference of
the two quadratic objectives, so discarding the two nonpositive terms gives
\[
 \langle f,(P_1-bP_3)g\rangle
 \le u^2+b\|P_3k\|_2^2
 \le u^2+\frac{18}{25}\left(\frac{849}{1000}\right)^2.
\]
Since \(|k|\le1\), the projection bound applies. On the complete low-amplitude
range \(0\le u\le21/100\), this gives the exact cap
\[
 \frac{441}{10000}+\frac{6487209}{12500000}
 =.56307672<\frac{40}{71},
\]
with a positive margin about .00030356169. The amplitude here is the actual
\(L^2\) first-projection norm, not divided by \(\sqrt{2/\pi}\).
